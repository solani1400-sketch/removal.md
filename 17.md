15 Abstract
16 The abuse of antibiotics is becoming increasingly serious, particularly in offshore
17 aquaculture areas. Double-network polymer gel has good prospects for environmental
18 application for the removal of antibiotics. In this work, κ-Carrageenan/Sodium
19 Alginate(κ-car/SA) double-network hydrogels were designed and synthesized with
20 enhanced mechanical properties, anti-swelling, and adsorption capacity. It was found
21 that the intermolecular interaction and viscosity tend to increase with the increasing
22 concentration of κ-carrageenan. The swelling degree of the composite hydrogel in NaCl
23 solution presented a decreasing trend with the increase of carrageenan. SA can
24 effectively improve the mechanical properties of κ-carrageenan composite gel and

---

2
25 enhance its compressive resistance and elasticity. Ciprofloxacin (CIP) was used as the
26 model pollutant for testing the adsorption performance. The results show that the
27 Langmuir-Freundlich isotherm model is more suitable for fitting the adsorption
28 isotherm data of CIP on gel beads, which indicates that κ-car/SA hydrogels have
29 heterogeneous surface and different binding sites. κ-car/SA composite double-network
30 hydrogels exhibit excellent adsorption properties for CIP (229 mg/g). The optimal
31 adsorption capacity of κ-car/SA composite hydrogels was obtained at pH 4, and the
32 adsorption capacity of the hydrogels increased with increasing ion concentration. FTIR
33 spectroscopy and the Zeta potential test analyses showed that the adsorption mechanism
34 may be explained by hydrogen bonding and the electrostatic interactions between κ-
35 car/SA composite hydrogels and CIP. The formation of the new double-network
36 hydrogel provided good properties and development potential for the adsorption of
37 antibiotic in water.

118 2.2 Fabrication of κ-car/SA beads
119 κ-car/SA beads were prepared by calcium-hardening method. In a typical
120 synthesis, certain amounts of κ-car and sodium alginate were added to a fixed volume
121 of DI water. The solid-liquid ratio was fixed at 2.5%, and the mass ratio of κ-
122 carrageenan to sodium alginate in each 100 ml of deionized water was 0.5 g:2 g, 1 g:1.5
123 g, 1.5 g:1 g, and 2 g: 0.5 g. In other words, the mass ratios of κ-carrageenan to sodium
124 alginate were 1:4, 2:3, 3:2 and 4:1. Then, the mixture solution was placed into a blender
125 and stirred for 5 hours. After stirring, the mixed solution was dripped into 1% calcium
126 chloride solution by a peristaltic pump and soaked for 8 hours. Finally, the κ-car/SA
127 beads were soaked in deionized water for 8 hours.

202 3.2 Swelling properties and mechanical properties of κ-car/SA beads
203 As shown in Fig. 3a, the degree of expansion of the κ-car/SA beads in water
204 increased with increasing κ-carrageenan content. When κ-carrageenan content
205 increased from 0.5 to 2 g, the swelling rate of the κ-car/SA beads in water increased
206 significantly (from 12% to 65%). However, in 3.5% and 5.8% NaCl solutions, the
207 swelling degree of composite hydrogels showed a decreasing trend (from 62% to 7%)
208 and was lower than that in water. Generally, the swelling degree of κ-car/SA beads in
209 water increases with the increase in the carrageenan fraction. The hydrogels were
210 prepared into short cylindrical shapes with a diameter of 1 cm and height of 1 cm.
211 Hydrogels with different proportions were manually extruded as shown in Fig. 3b. A
212 higher content of sodium alginate enables the gel to withstand greater pressure and
213 increases its resilience. On the other hand, the gel balls with the best compressive
214 resistance have very low adsorption capacity and removal rate of ciprofloxacin, and

321 cross-linking with calcium chloride and were shown to have a low swelling rate, high
322 mechanical properties, and excellent CIP adsorption characteristics. It was found that
323 κ-carrageenan can effectively reduce the swelling degree of sodium alginate composite
324 gel in NaCl solution and improve the adsorption capacity (229 mg/g) and removal rate

329 significantly enhanced. The characteristic peaks of carrageenan and sodium alginate
330 were retained in the FTIR spectra, indicating that the two compounds were successfully
331 cross-linked. The adsorption process of CIP on the adsorbent was studied, and the
332 interaction between the gel and the target was studied by Zeta potential analysis. The
333 results showed that the pH of the solution had a strong effect on the adsorption process
334 of κ-car/SA beads because CIP existed in different from at different pH. In addition,
335 carboxyl groups on the double-network gel beads also play an important role. This
336 study demonstrates improved adsorption, anti-swelling and mechanical properties of
337 natural polymer gels and provides a practical and theoretical basis for the development
338 of other systems.
