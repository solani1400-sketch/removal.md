### Abstract
This study examines time-varying adsorption of aqueous tetracycline (TC) by metal-organic
framework, MOF-5. The employed MOF-5 is firstly synthesized under room temperature
condition. Its structural, chemical, and physical features are then determined applying FTIR,
# ACCEPTED MANUSCRIPT

---

3
XRD, SEM, TGA, N2 adsorption, and TEM characterization techniques. Cubic arrangement,
84.3% crystallinity, up to 450ºC thermal stability and approximately 2510 m
2
/g BET surface
area with the both micro- and meso-cavities are its revealed characteristics. The impacts of the
operative factors (the adsorbent dosage, the contact time, the initial TC concentration, the initial
pH and the temperature) on TC removal efficiency are examined following a two-step
experimental design procedure (two-level categorical screening followed by response surface
methodology for correlative model development). Additionally, to define the most appropriate
condition/conditions, model-based optimization is implemented and verified in practice (more
than %96 TC removal is achieved). Further thermodynamic and kinetic assessments
respectively signify the both physisorption (Langmuir adsorption isotherm with maximum
capacity of 233 mg/g) and chemisorption (pseudo-second-order model) mechanisms in this
adsorption system. To sum up, MOF-5 as a suitable agent will possibly have significant effects
on pharmaceutical wastewater treatment and can be considered for advanced studies as an
appropriate candidate regarding practicable remediation techniques.
Keywords: MOF-5; Tetracycline antibiotic; Design of experiments; Adsorption;

### 1. Introduction:
Nowadays, water quality is a serious apprehension that must be resolved to preserve the natural
ecosystems for the time being and for future generations [1, 2]. One of the most important
groups of pollutants of concern consists of the pharmaceutical compounds. Huge amounts of
pharmaceuticals including antibiotics, hormones and anticonvulsants are globally released to
waterways every day [3, 4].

Tetracycline (TC) is a broad-spectrum antibiotic extensively utilized for human and animal
healthcare [5]. It has a high molecular weight with a chemical structure including several
carbonyl, amino and hydroxyl functional groups (Fig. 1). The emergence of TC came back to
1948 when Benjamin Duggar observed its production by Streptomyces aureofaciens bacterium
[6]. It has been utilized to treat a number of microbial infections such as anthrax, chlamydial
infections, community-acquired pneumonia, Lyme disease, cholera, syphilis, etc. [7]. It is also
used as veterinary medicine for farm animals and domestic pets [8]. These days, due to its
effectiveness and low cost, TC becomes one of the most broadly-exploited antibiotics in the
whole world [9]. According to the statistics, almost 5500 tons of TC are consumed annually in
US and Europe, and it had global sales of $1.6 billion in 2009 [10, 11].
Fig. 1
Excretion of the substance in form of urine, manure and feces of human and animals, in addition
to improper disposal of the expired medicines, and remainders left after drug abuse treatments,
contribute to TC entrance in surface water [12]. The presence and passage of TC in waterways
is of a great concern. For the reason that TC contributes to increase of microbial resistance to
similar antibiotics and consequently, problematic and even impossible removal of the mutant
infectious strains when the established wastewater treatment processes get employed [13]. In
an even catastrophic scenario, entrance of such strains to a host body may result in untreatable
illnesses that cannot be cured by use of conventional therapies [14]. Furthermore, TC can cause
gastrointestinal irritation, vomiting, diarrhea, and moreover, it can be absorbed into the bones
and may lead to a stable calcium complex which prevents the bones growth [4]. According to
the studies of Perez et al., TC has been detected in some surface waters in the range of 0.11-
4.20 𝜇𝑔 𝐿⁄ [15]. Importantly, the presence of traces of TC in the environment can lead to the
appearance of microorganisms that are resistant to the same antibiotics, to which humans may

be exposed via drinking water [16]. Therefore, this problem must be addressed by an effective
technological solution.
Scientists have studied the removal and degradation of TC by several methods such as
flocculation and coagulation, photo degradation, chemical oxidation and adsorption. These
approaches have numerous special drawbacks. For instance, large quantities of sludge are
produced applying the coagulation techniques; expensive reactants are required employing the
chemical oxidation methods; and high amounts of adsorbents are requisite exploiting the
adsorption processes for removal of aqueous pollutants [1, 17]. However, in comparison to the
other ones, the main advantages of the adsorption processes are the high removal efficiencies
and synthesis of non-toxic by-products [10, 18]. Clay [19], montmorillonite [20], chitosan
particles [21], activated carbon [22, 23], graphene oxide [24], palygorskite [25] and marine
sediments [26] are the most common adsorbents have been employed and tested for TC
adsorption. In spite of the mentioned impediment of adsorption processes, there is still a
growing request for the progress of effectual and economical treatment technologies for TC
removal from the effluents.
During the last decades, the study of metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) as new crystalline
materials with high adsorption capacity, tunable pore sizes, extremely high surface areas, high
reversibility and fast kinetics, has attracted significant interest [27-32]. Due to the exclusive
properties of MOFs, they have been extensively studied in several fields such as molecular
separations, heterogeneous catalysis, gas capture and storage [33]. MOF materials also offer
tremendous potential for adsorption of pollutants in wastewater treatment [34].
Up to date, several different MOFs such as MIL-101(Cr), MOF-235, MOF-74, UiO-67(Zr),
MOF-808, MOF-177, MOF-199, IRMOF-0, NU-1100, and ZIF-67 have been utilized for
different applications such as the removal of variety of contaminants from waters and
wastewaters. For instance, MOF-235 was applied for the elimination of methyl orange and

MOF-5 is one of the prominent specimens of MOFs owing to its high surface area and relatively
large pores. Li and coworkers were the first research group who synthesized and studied MOF-
5 [43]. It is famous for its hydrogen-storage behavior [33, 34]. Moreover, MOF-5 is applicable
for CO2 capturing [44] and adsorption of ammonia [45]. Also, MOF-5 is utilized for wastewater
treatment such as the removal of copper ions from aqueous media [46]. In contrast to hydrogen-
storage characteristic of MOF-5, its capacity for wastewater treatment and adsorption of
different contaminants from aqueous solutions has not yet been extensively investigated.
The main objectives of the current study are to examine the MOF-5 synthesis in room
temperature, investigate its utilization as an adsorbent for TC removal in a time-varying
process, and develop a model to determine the TC removal efficiency. To verify the adsorption
potential of MOF-5, the impacts of different operative variables (initial pH, initial TC
concentration, temperature, adsorbent dosage and contact time) as well as their interactions on
TC removal efficiency are explored. Accordingly, a two-step experimental design procedure is
applied. For this purpose, firstly, the significant factors are identified via a multi-level
categorical screening. Afterwards, response surface methodology (RSM) based on the central
composite design (CCD) is employed to more precisely pinpoint the effects of the independent
factors as well as their interactions and to develop an algebraic correlative model. Furthermore,
to define the most appropriate condition/conditions, model-based optimization is conducted

2.4. Experimental design and statistical evaluation
Suitable experimental design approaches are more preferable than one-factor-at-a-time method
[49, 50] owing to their abilities to reveal the possible interactions between the considered
factors based on a limited number of experiments [51]. A proper design of experiment (DoE)
consists of two main phases: the screening and successively the correlative model development.
2.4.1. Screening
In the screening step, one can determine whether the variable under study is significant or not.
In fact, preliminary screening tests can provide primary insights about the importance of the
individual factors as well as the ones which may have probable interactions. For this purpose,
by using Design Expert 10 software (https://www.statease.com/software/softwarehome.html),
a set of experiments was performed. The two-level screening tests for five considered variables
containing the initial pH, the initial TC concentration, the temperature, the adsorbent dosage
and the contact time were performed. The standard notations used in the two-level screening
tests are +1 and -1 denoting “high” and “low” levels for each factor. In other words, these
values are related to the first and the last points of the defined range for each variable. After
performing 2
𝑘 
experiments (k is the number of the considered variables), the obtained removal
efficiency from each test was introduced to the software. For analyzing these results, the
software provides analysis of variance (ANOVA) table which is a strong tool for realizing the
significant variables. Regarding ANOVA, the two parameters namely the probability-value (p-
value) and the Fisher- value (F-value), are precisely helpful. A p-value less than 0.05 is the
criterion for a factor with a significant effect on the process performance index [52]. On the

The adsorption/desorption isotherm diagram for the produced MOF-5 is depicted in Fig. 7. In
order to find an adsorption isotherm, the amount of the adsorbed gas at different relative
pressures (0.02-0.99) and a constant temperature of 77 K was measured. According to Fig. 7,
the nitrogen gas isotherm for MOF-5 is of type I/IV. The isotherm type I (often called the
Langmuir type) has a steep slope at the beginning of the nitrogen uptake process. With increase
of the relative pressure, this slope decreases significantly. Due to strong interactions between
the pore walls and the adsorbate, the adsorption took place at very low relative pressures [69].
This isotherm is rarely seen in mesoporous materials and is suitable for adsorbent with micro-
cavities (less than 2 nm). Also, the isotherm type IV is attributed to the presence of meso-
cavities. At low relative pressures, formation of a monolayer of the adsorbed molecules is the
prevailing process, while at high relative pressures, a multilayer adsorption takes place. Most
oxides are used as carriers and most catalysts belong to this class of solids [69]. In the light of
the facts outlined above, it is safe to say that the synthesized MOF-5 is consist of the both
micro- and meso-cavities. In addition, the existence of hysteresis in Fig. 7 can be used to
provide useful pieces of information associated with the geometry of the cavities. According

4. Conclusion
In the present study, MOF-5 was synthesized by the room temperature method. Different
characterization tests such as FTIR, XRD, SEM, EDX, EDS, TGA, BET, and TEM were
utilized to determine its properties. The results indicated 2510 m
2
/g BET surface area with
mesoporous structure, cubic structure and thermal stability up to 450℃. In order to evaluate its
adsorptive properties, it was applied for aqueous TC removal. The effects of different variables,
namely, the adsorbent dosage, the contact time, the initial TC concentration, the initial pH and
the temperature were examined. The screening tests revealed that the all variables were
significant enough to be included for further considerations in the correlative model
development step. In this step, a quadratic correlative model was developed, and formerly
optimized and validated by numerous experimental tests. The results of the equilibrium
experiments represented that the Langmuir isotherm model with the maximum adsorption
capacity of 233 mg/g was best fitted to experimental data. Feasibility, spontaneity, endothermic
nature and physisorption are the most important outcomes of the thermodynamic study. Also,
the kinetic of TC adsorption is well described by pseudo-second order kinetic model which led
to chemisorption. Thereby, the adsorption follows the both chemical and physical natures.

From research point of view, examining the capability of MOF-5 for TC removal deserves
superior attention. Especially, the both physisorption and chemisorption mechanisms of this
adsorption system provide a considerable opportunity for novel practicable remediation
techniques. All in all, it is obvious that MOF-5 is an efficient adsorbent to remove TC from
pharmaceutical wastewaters and can be considered as a proper agent for further studies.
However, for the time being, synthesis of MOFs is to some extent expensive and the related
studies are remarkable from fundamental scientific points of view. Conversely, we hope that
in future new methods will be found for MOFs synthesis which make them cost-effective
