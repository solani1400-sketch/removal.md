A B S T R A C T
In comparison with the intravenous route, oral administration is most commonly used owing to the non-invasive
nature and the fact that avoids patient pain and discomfort. In this context, UiO-66 was prepared through
solvothermal route to load tramadol (Tr) as a model drug. Tr loaded UiO-66 (Tr@UiO-66) was used to improve
the drug release controllability of bio-polymeric k-Carrageenan (k-Cr) hydrogel in the gastrointestinal tract (GIT)
conditions. The k-Cr/Tr@UiO-66 bio-nanocomposite hydrogel beads were characterized by FT-IR, XRD, EDX,
pHpzc, and SEM methods. The in-vitro drug release and kinetics analysis revealed that the k-Cr/Tr@UiO-66 has a
better performance against stomach pH and enhanced the drug dosing stability with controlled releases in the
GIT conditions. Cytotoxicity study established that the bio-nanocomposite beads have a cytocompatible nature
against human colon cells. Results showed that the prepared novel k-Cr/Tr@UiO-66 could be potentially used as
a nontoxic oral delivery vehicle.
1. Introduction
Today, controlled drug release owing to their benefits such as safety,
biocompatibility, biodegradability, sustained-release time and reduced
drug poisoning effect has concerned more attention in medicine [1,2].
In improving drug safety and efficacy via controlling the rate, time, and
place of drug release in the body, drug delivery system (DDS) plays a
critical role [3,4]. DDS is a device or a formulation that facilitates the
introduction of a therapeutic substance in the body. An ongoing chal-
lenge in bioengineering is finding an effective DDS for therapeutic
agents. The therapeutics oral administration, which internalized
through the body at the mouth, stomach, small intestine or colon, in
many cases does not provide such perfect delivery. Hence, materials,
technologies, and strategies have developed to control drug delivery by
slowing, delaying, or delivering to a specific area of the GIT [2,5,6].
Chronic pain is unlikable energy occurring majorly due to tissue
damage, outlook, influenced by thinking, behavior, community factors,
and responsible for causing psychological and emotional discomfort. Tr
for the treatment of pain as a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug is
commonly used and possess effective analgesic activity [7,8]. The
conventional oral delivery of Tr as a desirable choice for pain relief
need to manage due to their erratic absorption through the GIT [9].
Although, the interest area of many researchers is the modification of Tr
release [10–12], the organization of its sustained delivery to the oral
route has not been well planned, yet. From global health care, patient
compliance, perspective and financial, it is necessary to find a novel
methodology for oral administration of Tr with well therapeutic effi-
cacy. Due to the free solubility of Tr in water, to achieve a controlled
drug release, careful selection of release-retarding formulations is es-
sential. Currently, various agents as DDS, such as micro-capsule [13],
dendrimers [14], liposomes [15], nanomaterials [16] and natural or
synthetic polymeric particles [17] have attracted more consideration as
drug carriers [18,19].
For delivery and controlled release, in search of suitable matrices,
biopolymers as raw materials have been generally used owing to their
unique characteristics, for example, biocompatibility, biodegradability,
non-toxicity, and environmental sensitivity [20,21]. k-Cr, as a family of
sulfated linear polysaccharides of 3,6-anhydro-D-galactose and D-ga-
lactose, has exclusive features such as biocompatibility, hydrophilicity,
gel-forming, and non-toxicity characteristics [22,23]. Application of k-
Cr-based hydrogels in various arenas make them a great candidate for
biomedical applications [6,24]. Hydrogels are three-dimensional cross-
linked polymers (linear or branched) with hydrophilic features can
absorb large amounts of water, physiological solutions, and saline in

Recently, for drug delivery as favorable candidates, metal-organic
frameworks (MOFs) because of the tailorable pore size, large pore vo-
lumes, and high surface areas have become popular through author-
izing the design of biodegradable and biocompatible systems [31,32].
MOFs are composed of clusters or metal ions that linked via multitopic
organic ligands and creating extended network structures [33]. In
comparison with the conventional inorganic and organic DDS, the
MOF-based drug carriers show high loading capabilities and the po-
tential chemically modifying that can improve the drug affinity. Up to
now, biocompatible nanoscale MOFs, for instance cyclodextrin-based
MOFs [34], iron(III) carboxylate MOFs [35], zeolitic imidazolate fra-
meworks [36,37] and zirconium-containing MOFs [38] are great can-
didates for DDS. Among these, UiO-66 possesses high porosity and high
thermal, mechanical, and chemical stability [39–41].
In continuous of our interest research program on the development
of DDS with controlling the drug release [18,27,42] and porous mate-
rials [43–45]; herein, we wish to report an efficient oral vehicle via
composition of a bio-polymeric carrier with the nano-encapsulating
agent. We present the development of k-Cr as an oral drug delivery
using a novel combination of k-Cr and UiO-66 in a well-organizing
method. This biocompatible polysaccharide with special properties in
the GIT conditions could convey the drug-loded UiO-66 over the di-
gestive system. For the investigation of the controlled oral delivery of
Tr, the performance of the prepared system was studied in the simu-
lated GIT conditions.
2. Experimental
2.1. Materials
Zirconium (IV) chloride, ZrCl4, terephthalic acid, k-Cr, potassium
chloride (KCl) and Tr were purchased from Sigma–Aldrich Co. All sol-
vents used for the experiments were of analytical grade and used
without further purification and all other materials obtained from
Merck Co.
2.2. Characterization
UV–Vis absorption spectra were obtained by Analytic Jena (Specord
210) UV–vis spectrophotometer. Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR)
spectra were made in KBr pellets on a Shimadzu IR470 spectrometer. A
LEO 1430VP scanning electron microscopy (SEM) was used to de-
termine the surface morphology of the samples. Powder X-ray diffrac-
tion (XRD) measurements were performed at room temperature on a
STO diffractometer using Cu-Kα radiation at 35 kV in the scan range of
2θ from 5 to 70°. The pHs were measured with a Philips PW 9422 pH
meter.
2.3. Synthesis of UiO-66
According to the previously reported method [46], UiO-66 was
prepared by dissolving of ZrCl4 (0.053 g, 0.227 mmol) and BDC
(0.034 g, 0.227 mmol) in DMF (24.9 g, 340 mmol) at room temperature.
The resulting mixture was treated in a Teflon lined stainless steel re-
actor at 120 °C for 24 h. After crystallization under static conditions, the
reaction mixture was washed several times with DMF and dried at room
temperature.
2.4. Tr loading in the UiO-66
Typically, 100 mg of UiO-66 was soaked in drug solution (100 mg
Tr, in 10 mL water) and then shacked (150 rpm) at room temperature
for 72 h. The solvent was removed by centrifuging and washed with
distilled water to remove the unloaded drug. Tr@UiO-66 was dried in
the vacuum oven for 24. By using UV–Vis spectroscopy, the amount of
loaded drug in the UiO-66 was measured. To estimate the drug content
in the UiO-66, the free amount of Tr in the solution was collected and
then the amount of drug determined via the standard Tr calibration
curve at λ = 271 nm (Fig. S1). Eq. (1) and Eq. (2) were respectively
used to determine the loading capacity (LC) and efficiency (LE).

2.5. Preparation of k-Cr/Tr and k-Cr/Tr@UiO-66
For the preparation of k-Cr/Tr and k-Cr/Tr@UiO-66, 0.01 g of Tr
and 0.02 g of Tr@UiO-66 were separately dispersed into 10 mL distilled
water. Subsequently, 10 mL of a solution containing 0.2 g k-Cr gradu-
ally and individually was added to each of the prepared first solutions.
To ensure the complete dispersion of Tr and Tr@UiO-66 between the
polymer chains, the resulted mixture was stirred at room temperature.
Then, using a syringe (2 mm diameter), the solution was extruded in the
form of droplets, into 20 mL of KCl (0.01 M). The beads were kept in the
solution for 20–30 min to crosslink with K
+
. The beads were filtered
and washed with distilled water to remove un-reacted K
+
. Finally, the
beads were dried under vacuum for 24 h (Fig. 1).

2.8. In-vitro drug release studies
According to the previously reported procedure [49,50], in simu-
lated GIT conditions, the in-vitro drug release tests carried out with
some modifications. To determine in-vitro drug release, firstly, in a
dialysis membrane bag (MWCO = 6000, Sigma-Aldrich), 0.02 g of

time intervals, adequate amounts of samples were removed and to
maintain the volume of buffer in a constant value the same amount of

2.9. Cytotoxicity study
In order to evaluate the biocompatibility, MTT assay was assessed
for in-vitro cell toxicity of samples. Briefly, according to the typical
procedure [52], the human colon cell line was selected and was seeded
in 96-well plates at a density of 5000 cells/well in 200 μL cell medium
and incubated overnight. Then the resultant was treated with samples
with different concentration for 24 h and 48 h. After removing the
culture medium of each well, 20 μL MTT solution (2 mg/mL) was added
to them. After 4 h incubation at 37 °C the culture medium was removed
and then 200 μL DMSO was added to solubilize the formazan crystals.
The optical density (OD) was measured using a microplate reader
(Elx808, Biotek, USA) at a wavelength of 570 nm and the results were
compared with control cells containing only cell cultured medium;
besides, the culturing experiments were performed in triplicate.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Preparations of k-Cr/Tr@UiO-66
Fig. 1 illustrates the synthesis of UiO-66 and a proposed schematic
for stacking of Tr molecules in the UiO-66 porous geometry that
roughly corresponds to the expected experimental result. Indeed, Tr
applied as a model drug since its size is smaller than pores of UiO-66.
Then, via coating of Tr@UiO-66 with the k-Cr biopolymer, the pre-
paration of k-Cr/Tr@UiO-66 bio-nanocomposite hydrogel beads as a
novel carrier for oral delivery of anti-inflammatory drug was proposed.
In aqueous solutions of potassium chloride, because of the presence of
sulfate groups (-OSO3
-
) in the k-Cr structure, it can simplify complex to
the potassium ions and produce a network polymer [53]. The material
with a spherical shape, k-Cr/Tr@UiO-66 hydrogel beads, were shaped
by dropping a mixture solution of k-Cr and Tr@UiO-66 in the KCl so-
lution owing to the physical cross-linking. This technique is simple and
economically not require any other tools or energy for the coating of
various agents.

3.3. XRD analysis
The XRD patterns of UiO-66, Tr, Tr@UiO-66, k-Cr, k-Cr/Tr and k-
Cr/Tr@UiO-66 in the 2θ range of 5–70° are shown in Fig. 3a. The XRD
pattern of UiO-66 is in a good verification with the previous works [46].
As the XRD pattern of Tr@UiO-66 in Fig. 3a shows, the material con-
tains a crystalline mixture of UiO-66 and Tr [56], which attributed to
the high inclusion of Tr in the UiO-66, as the results were reported in
previous studies [57–59]. Moreover, the structural integrity of UiO-66
was retained in the hosting Tr [28]. XRD pattern of the k-Cr has the
most intense diffractions at 2θ = 28.24° and 40.43° in diffraction
patterns which associated with the diffractions of k-Cr hydrogel [60].
Besides, it can be observed that in the XRD pattern of k-Cr/Tr@UiO-66
the coating of Tr@UiO-66 is not altered the diffraction pattern of the k-
Cr hydrogel, which indicating an amorphous nature of bio-nano-
composite.
3.4. pHpzc and EDX analysis
The surface charge of particles is generally determining via the pH
of adsorbate solution, and that indicate the materials net surface charge
in the solution. It is well-known, when the pH of adsorbate solution is
higher than the corresponding pHpzc, the net charge on particles surface
is negative, and when the pH of adsorbate solution is lower than pHpzc,
the net charge on particles surface is positive [61]. So, to realize the
materials composition, the pHpzc analysis can be considered as available
evidence. As seen in Fig. 4, the pHpzc of UiO-66, k-Cr/Tr, and k-Cr/Tr@
UiO-66 were 2.4, 3.5, and 3.7, respectively. The composition of the
UiO-66 and k-Cr was gradually reduced the pHpzc of the k-Cr/Tr@UiO-
66 bio-nanocomposite. Furthermore, the successful formation of the k-
Cr/Tr@UiO-66 bio-nanocomposite with high purity was investigated by
the EDX spectrum, which only presented the presence of C, O, S and Zr
(Fig. 3b). These analyses could be good evidence for the successful
preparation of k-Cr/Tr@UiO-66 bio-nanocomposite hydrogel beads.

3.6. Swelling study
Another main feature that affecting the application of the hydrogels
is the swelling percentage. The swelling amount is affected via various
parameters, for instance, temperature, pH, ionic strength, composition,
and degree of crosslinking in the network [62]. The swelling behavior
of the bio-nanocomposite hydrogel beads was explained in the simu-
lated GIT conditions (Fig. 6a). At pH 1.2, as it is evident from the graphs
with time, the swelling ratio was radically increased. Compared to the
acidic medium by increasing the pH up to 6.8, the swelling ratio was
decreased. It seemed that owing to the electrostatic repulsion between
sulfate anions through deprotonating of the -SO3H groups (pKa 4.9) in
the k-Cr network, the swelling percentage must be increased while the
swelling test did not show this phenomenon. It is well-known, by
raising the pH of the buffered solution, the charge screening K
+ 
cations
generate a nonperfect anion-anion electrostatic repulsion among the
sulfate groups of k-Cr, and this decreases the swelling rate [63]. Be-
sides, the swelling ratio of k-Cr/Tr@UiO-66 somewhat is higher than k-
Cr/Tr hydrogel beads. Probably increasing the swelling capacity of the
k-Cr/Tr@UiO-66 is related to the polymeric network solidarity and
cavity (SEM results (Fig. 5)).
3.7. Tr loading and release
By using UV–Vis spectroscopy and Tr calibration curve, the EE%
and EC% of UiO-66 were calculated to be 82.8% and 45.3, respectively.
Due to the hydrophobicity, hydrogen bond, electrostatic attraction, and
the π-π interaction between the benzene ring of aromatic compounds
and the benzene ring of the organic ligand of UiO-66, a high adsorption
performance for aromatic compounds were observed [64]. Hence, the
various interactions between Tr and UiO-66 can be partially certified
this good EE. Also, this finding is evidenced by the fact that the XRD of
Tr@UiO-66 shows the contribution of Tr in the pattern (Fig. 3a).
In-vitro drug release experiments were performed to evaluate the
capability of the prepared bio-nanocomposite as an oral delivery ve-
hicle in the simulated GIT conditions. In this respects, the beads were
sequentially suspended in the medium with pH 1.2, 6.8, and 7.4 during
several special stages. The drug release profiles of the k-Cr/Tr and k-Cr/
Tr@UiO-66 are illustrated in Fig. 6b. In the simulated first zone of in-
testinal fluid (pH 1.2), the drug release rate was the lowest for k-Cr/
Tr@UiO-66. This can be justified by the more efficient attraction be-
tween UiO-66 and Tr at acidic pH. While, for k-Cr/Tr a high drug re-
lease rate can be observed at pH 1.2, which attributed to the high
swelling ratio. After that, in the intestinal fluid (pH 6.8 and 7.4) the
release rate dramatically increased for all samples, which might be
related to the ion exchange among the buffered solution (PO4
3−
) and
Tr. Tr@UiO-66 by well-organized interaction with Tr cause to the more
controlled release of drug in the GIT conditions; therefore, for this
purpose, k-Cr/Tr@UiO-66 bio-nanocomposite hydrogel bead has pro-
minence in comparison with k-Cr/Tr. The release data were examined
using several models (Table S1) to disclose the kinetics of drug release,
as described previously [51]. The Higuchi model gave the best fit for
the release date.

4. Conclusion
Briefly, UiO-66 was prepared via the solvothermal route, then Tr
was loaded in the UiO-66 by the immersing in the drug solution, and
finally, Tr@UiO-66 was coated with k-Cr biopolymer. Preparation of k-
Cr/Tr@UiO-66 bio-nanocomposite was proved by various analysis
techniques, including FT-IR, XRD, EDX, pHpzc, and SEM. Tr release tests
revealed that UiO-66 provides a better-controlled release of drug
through the GIT conditions. For k-Cr/Tr@UiO-66, sustained drug re-
lease was observed, which related to the unique features of k-Cr and
UiO-66. Cytotoxicity results proved that the prepared bio-nanocompo-
sites against human colon cells have a cytocompatible nature. Obtained
results revealed that the prepared novel k-Cr/Tr@UiO-66 could po-
tentially be used as a safe oral delivery vehicle.
