A B S T R A C T
Traces of antibiotics reaching aquatic environment lead to the emergence of antimicrobial resistance (AMR). The
efficient removal of antibiotics (ATBs) traces from wastewater is essential to tackle the AMR. In this study, a
novel solid-state crosslinking method of alginate (ALG) was developed and applied to specifically remove ATBs
from water. A wide range of crosslinkers (Ca
2+
, Zn
2+
, Cu
2+
, Ni
2+
, Fe
3+ 
and Al
3+
) was used and the crosslinking
nature, density, and distribution were evidenced by FTIR, ICP-MS, and SEM-EDS. Compared with ionotropic
gelation, the novel solid-state crosslinking method proved superior in term of ease of production, high cross-
linking degree, and ATBs removal capacity. Fe-ALG and Zn-ALG showed high removal capacity of ciprofloxacin
(356.5 mg/g and 928.6 mg/g) and doxycycline (90 mg/g and 690 mg/g), however, they were less effective
toward amoxicillin (11.5 mg/g and 6 mg/g). Removal isotherms and kinetics followed type I and pseudo-second
order suggesting a chemisorption removal mechanism. Fe-ALG was successfully regenerated with no loss in ATB
removal capacity. The microbiological assay showed significant reductions of antibacterial activities after ATBs
removal from water. Overall, metal-ALG systems obtained by solid-state crosslinking are promising for ATBs
removal from wastewater giving the ease of production, high efficiency, regenerability, and scalability potential.
1. Introduction
The discovery of antibiotics (ATBs) was arguably one of the greatest
medical achievements in 20th century. Since their discovery, millions of
tons of ATBs have been used globally saving millions human lives and
promoting livestock production. The overuse and misuse of ATBs inev-
itably accelerated the emergence and expansion of antimicrobial resis-
tance (AMR), which represents one of the biggest public health threats
that humankind must urgently resolve. By 2050, if no action is taken,
AMR can cause the death of 10 million lives a year and hundreds of
trillions of USD of economic cost (O’Neill, 2016). With practically no
new classes of ATBs created for decades novel strategies to extend the
useful life of existing ATBs has become a priority.
ATBs traces reaching environment are responsible, amongst other
reasons, for the emergence and spreading of AMR (Ben et al., 2019;
Khoder, Tsapis, Fattal, et al., 2010). The uncontrolled disposal of
contaminated wastewater generated by pharmaceutical industries,
hospitals, farms, and agriculture results in the accumulation of ATBs
residues in nature. This causes a selective pressure on environmental
bacterial species and consequently the evolution of the ecosystems and
the emergence of new AMR mutations (Ding & He, 2010; Manaia, 2017).
Preventing residual ATBs from reaching aquatic environments is
therefore a key action in the fight against the ever-growing AMR crisis
(Khoder, Tsapis, Domergue-Dupont, et al., 2010). Several strategies
were developed to remove ATBs traces from wastewater. This includes
oxidation (Hou, Chen, et al., 2019), flocculation (Hou, Du, et al., 2019),
ozonation and photocatalytic decomposition (Beltran et al., 2008),
enzyme degradation (Shao et al., 2019), adsorption (Yu et al., 2016), etc.

Most of these methods have limitations such as poor selectivity, sec-

synthesis, and scalability issue. Thus, new efficient, selective, cost-
effective, and scalable ATBs removal methods are still urgently needed
(Lu et al., 2020).
A number of ATBs classes, such as fluoroquinolones, β-lactams, and
tetracyclines, are known to form stable coordination complexes with
transition metals like Fe
3+
, Zn
2+ 
and Cu
2+ 
(Khurana et al., 2021). ATBs-
metal complexes are soluble in water and in most cases possess
enhanced ATBs antimicrobial activities (Cuprys et al., 2018; Khurana
et al., 2021). ATBs-metal complexation can be utilised to remove ATBs
from water if it takes place on a solid substrate, hence allowing ATBs
separation. Alginate (ALG) is a naturally occurring polysaccharide that
is safe, biocompatible, and widely used for different pharmaceutical,
biomedical and environmental applications. ALG itself is soluble in
water, therefore has no adsorption capacity toward ATBs. The abundant
carboxylate groups on ALG backbone form coordinate bonds with
transitional metals such as Ca
2+
, Fe
2+
, Zn
2+
, and Cu
2+ 
leading to the
formation of a crosslinked structure known as egg-box model (Dalaty
et al., 2016; Khoder et al., 2019). Upon crosslinking, ALG turns into an
insoluble salt where metals, acting as crosslinking nodes, could be
employed to form complexes with ATB molecules (Zhang, Lin, Ding, He,
et al., 2019). Crosslinked metal-ALG is traditionally produced in form of
beads by ionotropic gelation, where the crosslinking of ALG chains takes
place in a liquid-state (Dalaty et al., 2016). Although simple in concept,
the ionotropic gelation method is tedious involving the dissolution of
raw materials (i.e., ALG and metal salt) followed by bead formation,
curing, collection, washing, and drying. This renders the production of
metal-ALG less appealing from scalability perspective. Furthermore, in
the liquid-state, ALG chains are diluted with water, which distances
carboxylate groups preventing complete crosslinking. In the solid-state,
the ALG network is dense, bringing carboxylate groups into proximity,
hence more crosslinking is likely to happen. In the context of ATB
removal from water, a higher crosslinking density is highly desirable
since this is likely to enhance the maximum complexation capacity. So
far, to the authors’ knowledge, there were no previous studies reporting
the crosslinking of ALG in solid-state.
The aim of the present work is to develop for the first time a solid-
state ALG crosslinking method allowing the production of metal-ALG
with the least number of steps and highest crosslinking density. The
produced metal-ALG will be tested for the complexation and specific
removal of ATBs belonging to different classes from water. Several
divalent and trivalent transitional metals will be used as crosslinkers and
obtained metal-ALG will be characterized by FTIR, SEM, SEM-EDS, and
ICP-MS techniques to confirm crosslinking density and distribution and
establish the metal to ATB molar ratio after complexation. ATBs removal
capacity, isotherms, kinetics, and mechanism and the regeneration of
the metal-ALG system will be comprehensively investigated. Finally, the
reduction in water antibacterial activity will be investigated using agar
well-diffusion method.
2. Materials and methods
2.1. Materials
Sodium ALG (medium molecular weight: 120,000–200,000 g/mol;
M/G ratio: 0.8 (as provided by supplier) and sodium bicarbonate
(NaHCO3) were purchased from Sigma Aldrich chemicals (Dorset, UK).
Ciprofloxacin hydrochloride monohydrate (CIP) (>98 %), levofloxacin
(LEV) (98 %), norfloxacin (NOR) (98 %), tetracycline hydrochloride
(TCN) (97 %), doxycycline (DOX) (97 %), and cephalexin (CEX) (98 %)
were all purchased from Tokyo Chemical Industry Co (Tokyo, Japan).
Amoxicillin hydrochloride (AMX) (98 %) and ampicillin trihydrate
(AMP) (98 %) were obtained from ACROS organic (Leicestershire, UK).
Iron chloride anhydrous (FeCl3) (98 %), copper chloride (CuCl2) (98 %),
aluminium chloride (AlCl3) (98.5 %), nickel chloride (NiCl2) (98 %),
zinc acetate dihydrate, (Zn (CH3CO2)2) (98 %). Hydrochloric acid (HCl)
(analytical reagent grade) was purchased from Fisher scientific inter-
national (Leicestershire, UK). Zinc and iron single element calibration
standard solutions (1000 ppm) were all purchased from Merch (Darm-
stadt, Germany).
2.2. Preparation of metal-ALG powders
2.2.1. Ionotropic gelation method
Liquid-state crosslinking was produced by the conventional iono-
tropic method. Briefly, ALG solution (3 % w/v) was prepared in 100 mL
water and added dropwise to 300 mL of metal solutions at a concen-
tration of 10 % w/v. Different metals were used including CaCl2, Zn
(CH3CO2)2, CuCl2, NiCl2, FeCl3, and AlCl3. The resulting metal-ALG
beads were stirred in the metal salt solution for 30 min, collected,
washed several times (1 min each wash) in 300 mL deionized water, and
finally freeze-dried for 48 h (VirTis Benchtop Pro, SP scientific,
Winchester, UK). Metal-ALG powders obtained by the ionotropic gela-
tion method labelled L-Metal-ALG, where L indicates the liquid-state
crosslinking.
2.2.2. Solid-state crosslinking
Aqueous metal salt solutions (CaCl2, Zn (CH3CO2)2, CuCl2, NiCl2,
FeCl3, AlCl3) at a concentration of 2.75 mM were prepared and 2 mL of
the obtained solutions were added dropwise to 2.5 g ALG and mixed for
5 min using pestle and mortar. The resulting close-to-dry metal-ALG
powders were washed once with 400 mL deionized water for 20 min and
left to dry overnight. Metal-ALG powders obtained by the solid-state
crosslinking method were labelled S-Metal-ALG where S refers to
solid-state crosslinking.
2.3. Fourier-transform infrared spectroscopy (FT-IR)
FT-IR analysis of ALG, L-Fe-ALG, S-Fe-ALG, L-Zn-ALG, and S-Zn-ALG
samples was performed to confirm the crosslinking. The spectra were
acquired using an FT-IR spectrometer (Thermo fisher, Madison, USA) at
ambient temperature over a wavenumber range of 4000 to 600 cm

2.4. Determination of metal content
To determine the number of washings required to remove all free
(non-crosslinking) metal, the metal concentration in washing solutions
were quantified by the inductive coupled plasma mass spectrometry
(ICP-MS) (Agilent 7700 series, Tokyo, Japan). Zn
2+ 
and Fe
3+ 
calibration

10 mL nitric acid solution (2 % w/v) and quantified using ICP-MS. The

To determine the amount of metal involved in crosslinking, 50 mg
metal-ALG, containing no free metal, was incubated in 10 mL nitric acid
solution (50 % w/v) for 24 h to allow the complete digestion of powders
and the release of metal. The amount of released metal was quantified
using ICP-MS as explained above.

2.6. ATBs removal studies
2.6.1. Impact of crosslinker type and crosslinking method

2.8. Antibacterial assay
The antimicrobial activity was assessed using the agar well-diffusion
method against three bacterial strains: Escherichia coli NCTC 12241
(E. coli), Staphylococcus aureus NCTC 6571 (S. aureus), and Pseudomonas
aeruginosa NCTC00950 (P. aeruginosa). Mueller Hinton agar (MHA)
plates (5 mm thickness, Oxoid UK) were then prepared, and 8 mm wells
were cut out using a sterilised cork borer and sealed from the bottom
with 30 μL MHA. Briefly, overnight cultures were prepared on nutrient
agar plates and incubated for 24 h at 37 
◦
C. The bacteria were then
suspended in Ringer solution (Oxoid, UK) to a turbidity equivalent to a
0.5 McFarland standard (1.5*10
8 
CFU/mL). The bacteria inoculum was
then spread on the MHA plates using sterile cotton swabs and wells were
subsequently filled with 30 μL of sterilised CIP or DOX solutions (20 μg/
mL), before (control) or after ATBs removal was performed for 4 h using
S-Fe-ALG and S-Zn-ALG (10 mg/mL). The zones of inhibition were
measured in millimetres (mm). Upon completing the ATBs removal
experiment, S-Fe-ALG and S-Zn-ALG particles were collect, dried over-
night at 40 
◦
C, and the antibacterial activity of 3 mg of resulting powder
(i.e., S-Fe-ALG and S-Zn-ALG complexed with either CIP or DOX) was
assessed following the same method mentioned above.
2.9. Statistical analysis
Statistical significance was measured using the one-way analysis of
variance (ANOVA) and Student’s t-tests as appropriate. All experiments
were performed in triplicate and values were expressed as the mean ±
standard deviation. Values of P < 0.05 were regarded as significantly
different.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Metal-ALG preparation and characterisation
Crosslinked metal-ALGs have been employed for a variety of envi-
ronmental and pharmaceutical applications (Jadach et al., 2022; Thakur
et al., 2018). When employed for ATBs removal, ALG composites loaded
with adsorbents are usually used (Wang et al., 2018). The ionotropic
gelation is the predominant method of ALG crosslinking, during which
metal cations diffuse in ALG droplets leading to a spontaneous and
progressive sol–gel transition (Sriamornsak et al., 2008; Sriamornsak &
Kennedy, 2008). In the current study, a novel one-pot crosslinking
method of ALG in its solid-state was developed, avoiding dissolution,
multiple washings, and drying steps. A wide range of transitional metals,
namely Ca
2+
, Zn
2+
, Cu
2+
, Ni
2+ 
(divalent cations) and Fe
3+ 
and Al
3+
(trivalent cations) was used to crosslink the ALG by both ionotropic
gelation and solid-state crosslinking methods. Highly concentrated
metal solutions (10 % w/v and 2.75 mM for liquid-state and solid-state
crosslinking, respectively) were used to achieve the highest possible
degree of crosslinking, hence the highest ATBs removal capacities. Fig. 1
shows the visual appearance of metal-ALG powders. The powders col-
ours, regardless of the preparation methods, reflected the corresponding
metal colour, indicating the successful entrapment of metals by ALG
(Fig. 1A and B). The entrapment of metals was also confirmed using
SEM-EDS method (Fig. 1S). L-metal-ALG particles displayed sleek and
smooth surface (Fig. 1D), while S-metal-ALG one had rough surface
which was like that of unprocessed ALG (Fig. 1C and E). It is worth-
mentioning that contrary to starting materials, all obtained metal-ALG

3.5. Removal capacity, isotherms, and kinetics
Given their superior removal capacity and ease of preparation, solid
crosslinking methods was adopted for the rest of this study. Zn

calculated according to Eq. (1) and used to plot the corresponding
complexation-removal isotherms (Fig. 5). All isotherms fitted with type
I, suggesting a monolayer saturable complexation process which agrees
with mono- or bidentate ligand behaviour of fluoroquinolone, tetracy-
clines, and β-lactams toward metal chelation (Anacona, 2001; Khurana
et al., 2021; Uivarosi, 2013). S-Fe-ALG maximum removal capacities
toward CIP, DOX and AMX were 356.5 mg/g, 90 mg/g and 11.5 mg/g,
respectively. For S-Zn-ALG, the maximum removal capacities of CIP,
DOXY and AMX were around 928.6 mg/g, 690 mg/g and 1.2 mg/g,
respectively, which are superior to those reported for comparable ALG-

ALG were calculated: the higher the initial concentration of ATB solu-

3.6. Metal-ALG regeneration
The regenerability and useable lifetime of removal agents are key
characteristics that determine their scalability and application potential.
In this study, S-Fe-ALG particles were successfully regenerated up to
three times. After the saturation of all S-Fe-ALG complexation sites with
CIP, Fe 
3+ 
cations were extracted using a concentrated HCL solution (1
M), which led to the formation of insoluble alginic acid. The resulting
alginic acid particles were then treated with NaHCO3 solution to pro-
duce Na-ALG particles that were finally re-crosslinked by the solid-state
method to regenerate the S-Fe-ALG. Fig. 6 shows the removal percent-
ages of CIP (1500 μg/mL) using freshly prepared and regenerated S-Fe-
ALG particles. The removal capacity of regenerated S-Fe-ALG was
comparable to that of the freshly prepared S-Fe-ALG (P > 0.05), even
after the third cycle of regeneration.

4. Conclusion
Metal-ALG particles produced by ionotropic gelation and solid-state
crosslinking were successfully applied to complex and remove ATBs
from water. A very high crosslinking density was obtained by the new
solid-state crosslinking method allowing an abrupt and efficient removal
of fluoroquinolones and tetracyclines from water. A significant-to-
complete reduction of antimicrobial activity against gram-positive and
gram-negative bacterial species was evidenced using agar well-diffusion
method. The isotherms and kinetics of ATB removal were best fitted with
type I and pseudo-second order models that might indicate a chemi-
sorption removal of ATBs, which is in line with the metal complexation
hypothesis. Our work shows that metal-ALG particles produced by the
new solid-state crosslinking represent an efficient, environmentally
friendly, cost-effective, and regenerable material for the specific
complexation and removal of several classes of ATBs from water, hence
contributing to tackling the vicious cycle of AMR emergence and
spreading in aquatic environment.
