Zeolitic imidazolate framework-8 (ZIF-8), which consists of metal atom Zn and 2-methylimidazole, is a
kind of typical MOF materials. The unique properties of ZIF-8 endue it superior adsorption capacity
and good host ability for photocatalytic materials, which makes it be a high-profile material in the treat-
ment of wastewater. However, ZIF-8 particles tent to aggregate together and result in the decrease of
specific surface area. They also suffer from weak recyclability, as it is hard to separate them from water
after removing pollutant. The newly emerging ZIF-8 composite materials solve the above problems, and
are successfully applied in wastewater treatment. In this review, the current design and synthesis meth-
ods of ZIF-8 composites are systematically summarized. The recent research progress of ZIF-8 composites
as adsorbents and photocatalysts to remove wastewater pollutants is critically introduced. Different fac-
tors affecting its efficiency and the mechanism of pollutant removal are also discussed. This review
attempts to lay the mechanistic insight for the application of ZIF-8 composite in practical wastewater
treatment and provides new ideas for constructing high-efficient ZIF-8 based adsorbent and photocata-
lyst. For the purpose of revealing the whole potentiality of ZIF-8 composite in pollutants removal and
water regeneration, current challenges are introduced and future research directions are proposed at last.

1. Introduction
As one of environmental problems, water pollution seriously
endangers human health [1–3]. Thus, it is pretty significant and
urgent to control and treat water pollution. Water pollution results
from a wide range of sources that contain various complex compo-
nents. In the past years, hundreds of emerging organic contami-
nants (EOCs) that pollute water resources have been discovered,
including pharmaceuticals, personal care products, industrial pol-
lutants and so on [4,5]. Most EOCs, owing to their physical or
chemical properties (such as high-grade and water-soluble), can
hardly be completely removed by traditional water treatment
methods even at low concentrations [6]. In addition to EOCs, inor-
ganic pollutants are another major issue of human concern, their
carcinogenicity, mutagenicity, teratogenicity and toxicity will lead
to severe environmental effects and health menace. Thus, many
techniques and strategies have been proposed to purify wastewa-
ter, such as coagulation, filtration and so forth [7–10]. However,
due to the need for complex and large facilities with expensive
maintenance costs, these methods are not ideal [11]. In addition,
it is a big problem to convert the concentrated or separated pollu-
tants into nonpoisonous substances. When take energy and econ-
omy into consideration, it may be more promising to treat
pollutants by adsorption or photocatalytic reactions [12–15]. With
the purpose of obtaining a higher removal efficiency of pollutants,
researchers are exploring for novel materials to prepare adsorbent
and photocatalyst [16,17].
Metal-organic framework (MOF) is a kind of porous organic/
inorganic hybrid material, it is also known as porous coordination
polymers and has been intensively investigated as a fascinating
material for scientists and engineers [18]. It consists of inorganic
secondary building units (SBUs) of metal ions or oxo-clusters and
bridging organic linkers. The chemical composition and building
units shape of MOF can be altered within a specific structure that
already exist, which provide possibility for synergistic combination
of properties [19–22]. MOFs have the advantages of high surface
areas, ordered pore structure, high crystallinity and polymetallic
sites, and good compatibility with polymers [23–25]. The struc-
tural diversity and unique properties facilitate MOFs as attractive
materials for removing toxic metals, EOCs and other pollutants
from wastewater [26–29]. Zeolite-like imidazole skeleton material
(ZIFs) is a kind of (MOFs) with zeolite skeleton structure, which is
generated by reaction in solvent with Zn or Co as metal source and
imidazole or imidazole derivatives as organic ligands [30,31]. They
not only inherit the structural advantages of MOFs, but also over-
come the shortcomings of poor thermal and chemical stability of
MOFs to a certain extent [32–34]. Since their discovery in 2006,
ZIFs have been applied in many fields due to their outstanding
properties [32,35]. ZIF-8 is a typical ZIFs material, the team of Chen
group first synthesized it and named MAF-4 at that time [36].
Later, the Yaghi’ research group conducted a systematic study on
it and officially named it ZIF-8 [33]. Zn(Hmim)2 is the chemical for-
mula of ZIF-8, which consists of 2-methylimidazole and metal
atom Zn [37,38]. Compared with other MOF materials, the synthe-
sis strategies of ZIF-8 are richer and easier. ZIF-8 also has higher
chemical and hydrothermal stability that greatly profits from its
structure formed by robust links. Take its thermal stability as an
example, ZIF-8 can still maintain structural stability when the tem-
perature is up to 500 °C. Further, ZIF-8 can also keep its crys-
tallinity and porosity after being put into various solutions, such
as water and organic solvents [39–41]. ZIF-8 has easily tailorable
pore sizes and structures, which is beneficial to its modification.
Moreover, the development of mesoporous and multistage porous
ZIF-8 greatly expands the application range of traditional microp-
orous ZIF-8. There are numerous active sites and facial charge-
separation on the surface of ZIF-8. Now, ZIF-8 materials are widely
used in many forms including powders, colloid, membranes or thin
films, and have been applied to diverse important fields [42].
The unique physical structure and chemical properties of ZIF-8
provide a promising platform for its design and research in adsorp-
tion and photocatalytic systems, and making it a research hot pot
in the treatment of water pollutants. However, ZIF-8 always suffers
from these shortcomings: (i) ZIF-8 nanoparticles tend to aggregate
in water, which will lead to the increase of particle size and trans-
fer resistance, and the reduce of interfacial area, thus reduce their
adsorption performance; (ii) as ZIF-8 nanoparticles are difficult to
be separated from aqueous, their regeneration and reuse are
greatly restricted; (iii) ZIF-8 shows weak responsivity to visible
light due to its large band gap, and then influence its solar irradia-
tion efficiency. Furthermore, with the increasing demand for func-
tional materials, the application of ZIF-8 monomer is greatly
limited [43]. New multifunctional composites/hybrids can be
formed through the controllable integration of ZIF-8 and functional
materials, these composites or hybrids exhibit superior properties
than individual components through the collective behavior of
each functional units, and have attracted widespread attention
[44,45]. To date, ZIF-8 composites have been successfully prepared
with active species, such as fibers, metals, oxides, polyoxometa-
lates (POMs), polymers, graphene, carbon nanotubes (CNTs) and
so on (Fig. 1). They can maintain the original properties and func-
tions of each phase, and also make up for the limitation of any sin-
gle phase in application [46]. Compared with pure microporous
ZIF-8, ZIF-8 composite material not only enhances the adsorption
capacity and photocatalytic activity of ZIF-8, but also broadens

2. Rational design strategies of ZIF-8 composites
For the purpose of fabricating a variety of ZIF-8 composite
materials with synergistic property of guest and host components,
the rational fabrication of nanocomposite is a convenient and
effective approach [52–55]. It is worth noted that the precise con-
trol over the synthesis of ZIF-8 NPs is critical, as only the optimal
structure of ZIF-8 can ensure the localization of nucleation and
its subsequent growth around functional guests, creating compos-
ites unattainable by other techniques such as guest infiltration,
surface adsorption or covalent linkage [56]. Two categories of
single-particle ZIF-8 composites have been reported: encapsula-
tion of guest materials into ZIF-8 [57–59]; and post-synthetic
hybridization of pre-synthesized ZIF-8 with functional materials
[60,61].
There are two ways to encapsulate nanoparticles into ZIF-8. One
is the ‘‘ship-in-a-bottle” approach, which involves introducing pre-
cursors into the pre-synthesized ZIF-8 shells. In the synthesis pro-
cesses, ZIF-8 generally acts as an ideal self-sacrificial template, and
the precursor is then reduced or decomposed to produce particles
deposited in the cavity (Fig. 3). This encapsulation approach sim-
plifies the formation of interface between the particles and ZIF-8,
and make it possible to produce ultrasmall particles in the cavities
of ZIF-8. However, it is difficult to regulate the shapes of the
nanoparticles. Moreover, the pore sizes of ZIF-8 usually greatly
limit the grow of these particles, and the overloading of particles
will also cause damage to ZIF-8 framework. For instance, Li et al.
used the ‘‘ship-in-a bottle” method and successfully prepared
ZIF-8@Au [62]. They first synthesized ZIF-8 crystals, and later cre-
ated a single layer of Au NPs that equably distributed on the sur-
face of crystal. The other approach is the ‘‘bottle-around-ship” or
de novo approach, which involves loading ZIF-8 precursors on
the nanoparticles. The sizes of pre-synthesized particles are not
limited by the caves size of ZIF-8, moreover, this method achieves
flexible control the shape and composition of these particles. But
many reaction conditions should be taken into consideration, such
as the capping agents of particles or the interaction between parti-
cles and ZIF-8 [19]. In general, when the nanoparticles are in the
form of liquid nanodroplets, it is hard to achieve their proper sizes
distribution inside the cavities. While Liu et al. used the ‘‘bottle-a
round-ship” method and successfully encapsulated EGaIn nan-
odroplets into ZIF-8 cavities [63]. They firstly treated the EGaIn
nanodroplets with PVP, there were many ethylene moieties and
carbonyl groups on PVP, which could promote the crystallization
of ZIF-8 around EGaIn. The research of Liu et al. provided innova-
tive ideas for the further studied on liquid particles-based ZIF-8
composites. Among the already reported studies, besides using
the abovementioned encapsulation strategies to fabricate ZIF-8
composites, ZIF-8 can also embed multiple molecules through sim-
ple and low-cost physicochemical processes (Fig. 4a).
In this part, a series of synthesis methods are summarized, such
as in-situ growth method, surface modification method, template
synthesis method and so on. These strategies provide vital paths
for the preparation of various ZIF-8-based composites. According
to the properties of single-component materials or the application
requirements of composite materials, the synthesis method can be
flexibly selected to purposefully alter the compositions and prop-
erties of the composites. For clarity, various ZIF-8-based compos-
ites for wastewater treatment are summarized in Table 1.

2.2. Surface modification method
Surface modification method treats the surface of nuclear parti-
cles with some surfactants, coupling agents or crystal seeds, so as
to improve the adsorption and binding force between the nanopar-
ticles and the ZIF-8, then achieve the close combination of them.
This method has the advantages of simple operation and strong

Secondary growth method of seed crystal is a kind of surface
modification method, it uses nano-scale ZIF-8 seed particles
instead of chemical reagents to load and modify the surface of
the carrier particles, and accelerates the growth of ZIF-8 crystals
through the structure guiding effect of seed crystals, eventually
forming a dense and continuous ZIF-8 shell that cover the carrier.
This method can effectively weak the sensitivity of ZIF-8 crystals
to the synthesis conditions, meanwhile, the precipitation of crys-

this method to prepare mesoporous silica-(ZIF-8) spheres (labelled

as template, and the ZIF-8 grew uniformly around the MSS through
the seeding processes. The silica hydroxyls of MSS could interact
with Zn

hydrophilic mesoporous silica. Thermogravimetry analyses were

MSS-Z8 could still keep stable under more than 400 °C in open air.
Excepting ZIF-8 crystals, ZIF-8 film can also form around the tem-

will be dispersed in the spinnable solution and introduced into the

2.4. Other methods
Except for the main synthesis methods above, there are also
other novel synthesis strategies. Such as, sonochemical method,
microreactor synthesis method, stepwise growth (LPE), deposition
reduction method and so on. Zeng et al. easily prepared TiO2/ZIF-8
via sonochemical method [74]. Microreactor synthesis is also
another relatively novel synthesis strategy, compared with the tra-
ditional hydrothermal synthesis method, this method has the fol-
lowing advantages: (i) the specific surface area is larger, which is
conducive to the full contact between different reactants; (ii) the
thermal conductivity is far exceeding the traditional reactor, which
allows the micro-reactor to rapidly heat up or cool down in a short
time; (iii) the mass transfer rate is faster, making the mixing time
between reactant is greatly shortened; (iv) the synthesis condi-
tions are easy to adjust. Since the number of reactants required
in the microreactor is very small, the corresponding experiment
conditions like temperature, pressure, reactant flow rate and reac-
tion residence time can be easily adjusted. The LPE strategy previ-
ously is used to fabricate MOFs membranes on other materials, its
application in constructing ZIF-8 layers on powder substrates is
rarely investigated [103,104]. Among the already reported meth-
ods, it is a challenge to grow MOFs in restrained spaces. Shekhah
et al. first deposited MOF layers on modified bulk substrate by
LPE rout [105]. Through this method, they achieved feasible oper-
ation of MOF layers in ordered mesoscopic channels, and kept the
properties of each components at the same time. Later Peng et al.
studied from them and deposited ZIF-8 layers on SiO2 nano-
powders via this approach [106]. This strategy ensures the stable
grow of ZIF-8 around the substrate surface and protect the ZIF-8
from nucleation in precursor solution.
It is worth noting that in the practical production of the ZIF-8
composites, the synthetic strategies are nonmonotonic [107]. Take
Wu et al.’ study as an example, they used the surface modification
method and solvothermal method to fabricate Fe3O4@ZIF-8, poly-
styrene sulfonate sodium (PSS) was applied to modify the Fe3O4
particles, therefore to promote the nucleation and growth of ZIF-
8 on the surface of Fe3O4 [108]. The synthesized composites
demonstrated uniform microporous, high surface area as well as
good stability in water. Thus, with the purpose of obtaining ZIF-8
composites with optimal properties, different synthesis methods
can be used synergistically during the synthesis processes.
3. Application of ZIF-8 composites for removing pollutants from
wastewater
As a new type of sewage treatment material, owing to its excel-
lent properties, ZIF-8 composite is usually applied to efficiently
remove many types of wastewater pollutants as adsorbent and
photocatalyst, it has an immeasurable application prospect in the
field of wastewater regeneration.
3.1. ZIF-8 composites for the adsorptive removal of pollutants in
wastewater
Adsorption is a striking technique for treating wastewater, for
its facial operation, low cost and litter harmful byproducts. Explor-
ing the efficient sorbents is the major prerequisites for the adsorp-
tion process, then for a definite adsorbent, innovation in its
structure and morphology is an ideal way to increase the reaction
sites and then enhance its adsorption ability.
ZIF-8 has large specific surface area and high porosity, its rotat-
ing door effect endues it ability of adsorbing large molecules that
exceed its pore size. The experiment results of Yaghi group further
demonstrated that the modification and synthesis of ZIF-8 is sim-
ple, which lay good foundation for its integration with other mate-
rials to form a good adsorbent [33]. Compared with single ZIF-8,
ZIF-8 composites can (i) decrease the aggregation of adsorbent;
(ii) make it easier for the adsorbent to be separated from the water;
(iii) increased the active sites and stability of adsorbent which
exhibits superior adsorption ability and better recyclability to
those of the individual constituents during the treatment of water
pollutants [17,109].
Moreover, compared with the conventional or commercial
adsorbent materials, such as coconut shells, activated carbon, acti-
vated alumina, clay or hybrid materials, ZIF-8 composites possess
better tailorability, higher surface area and porosity. Meanwhile,
the active groups on the surface of it provide abundant adsorption
sites that can bind to various pollutants [110]. On the one hand, the
specific surface area of pure ZIF-8 NPs generally ranges from 1500-
2500 m
2
/g, which is higher than many other adsorbents. On the
other hand, with the synergy of functional materials (such as fibers
and porous NPs), the water stability, recyclability, porosity or pore
size of ZIF-8 composites will be further enhanced. Owing to the
high adsorption capability and long service life of ZIF-8 composites,
more and more researchers are trying to remove water pollutant
with various novel ZIF-8 composites. As it is well known that pol-
lutants produced from different sources will lead to different spe-
cies, among these species, emerging organic pollutants and
inorganic pollutants are the pollutants that currently applied more
to evaluate the removal efficiency of ZIF-8 composites [111–113].
3.1.1. Adsorptive removal of inorganic pollutants in wastewater
Radioactive substances, heavy metal ions and excess halide ions
are several typical inorganic pollutants [114]. Various common
heavy metal ions are often studied, such as arsenic, antimony, cop-
per, mercury, chromium, lead and nickel, they all cause great harm
to human and water environment [115–119]. With the increasing
consume of nuclear energy with the development of economy,
radioactive nuclear waste becomes another powerful enemy for
clean water. In particular, some radioactive substances have char-
acteristics of high solubility, long half-lives and environmental
mobility. Chernobyl-type leak and Fukushima’s Daiichi Nuclear
Power Plant are two typical examples [120]. As summarized in
Table 2, these inorganic pollutants are toxic or carcinogenic, if

but participate in the food chain cycle and eventually accumulate

performance. With the maturity of characterization technology
and ZIF-8 composite synthesis technology, ZIF-8 composites with
different structures and functions have been discovered. Therefore,
ZIF-8 composites have attracted great attention in the adsorptive
removal of water pollutants in recent years [121].

3.1.2. Adsorptive removal of organic pollutants in wastewater
The types of organic pollutants in wastewater are richer than
inorganic ones, including organic dyes, environmental priority
organic pollutants and PPCPs. Dye wastewater is one of the intract-
able wastewater at home and abroad due to its high COD, biologi-
cal oxygen demand and many refractory substances. Organic dyes
in the water will not only deepen the chroma of the water body,
affect the sense of the water body, but also block the transmission
of sunlight and consume the dissolved oxygen in the water body,
these will affect the growth of aquatic organisms, and even destroy
the entire ecosystem of the water body, so it needs to be handled
properly [140,141]. Typical recalcitrant dye has anionic MO, catio-
nic dye RhB, malachite green (MG), MB and so on [132,142]. Prior-
ity organic pollutants are difficult to degrade, they have a certain
level of residue in water and can be detected easily. PPCPs mainly
refer to drugs, fragrances, cosmetics, sunscreens, hair dyes, etc,
their concentrations are generally low in wastewater [143].
There have reported many ZIF-8 composites to remove dyes
from water as a promising method, such as Fe3O4@ZIF-8, ZIF-8/
PC-SMM, TiO2@ZIF-8 and so on [139,144]. Dai and co-workers used
the MG aqueous solution as the separation system to test the per-
formance of the modified ZIF-8 [145]. In this experiment, ZIF-8
grew equably on the surface of polyacrylate carboxyl super-
macroporous microspheres (PC-SMM) and formed ZIF-8/PC-SMM
composites. The fabricated ZIF-8/PC-SMM not only had the adsorp-
tion property of ZIF-8, but also presented spherical shape of PC-
SMM, so that the ZIF-8 nanoparticles can fully contact with MG
molecules. The BET characterization indicated that the specific sur-
face area of the ZIF-8/PC-SMM composites were 128 times that of
the PC-SMM microsphere. Since MG could not go through the
pores of ZIF-8 and reached the inner core PC-SMM, the adsorption
of MG mainly occurred on the surface of ZIF-8. MG could be

For the existing photocatalysts, their main problems are the rel-

treatment [151,152]. Hence it is significant to create photocatalysts
with enhanced activities, economical cost, especially visible light-

used as photocatalysis for their high conversion efficiency of solar

which limits the potential application of photocatalytic degrada-

potential in the design of novel photocatalytic material. Indian pro-
fessor Garcia in the University of Valencia proposed for the first
time that Zn4O(CO2)6(MOF-5) has photocatalytic activity and can
degrade phenol [156]. Later, ZIF-8 shows great potential, its
organic functional groups can provide a certain catalytic activity,
and its large pore volume makes it a catalyst carrier. When ZIF-8
works as catalysts, catalytically active particles are usually com-
bined with it, since ZIF-8 can promote mass diffusion of active par-
ticles, meanwhile, the porous structure makes itself a molecule
separator for selective catalysis.
When under the UV light, ZIF-8 with semiconductor properties
will be excited, and then generate electrons and holes, which is the
first reaction step of photocatalysis [157,158]. Util now, ZIF-8
nanocrystals have been applied to photocatalysis fields. However,
because of their large band gap (4.9–5.1 eV) and low electron dis-
charge ability, their photocatalysis performance is usually inferior
to traditional semiconductors. Therefore, various strategies are
developed to reduce the bandgap and increase the electron dis-
charge efficiency, such as combining ZIF-8 with metal oxides, using
different linkers or metal centres, or loading other functional mate-
rials with optical properties to obtain a composite with better pho-
tocatalytic activity [86,159].
3.2.1. ZIF-8 combined with semiconductors
Among the above strategies, semiconductor combination is con-
sidered to be a very common way to improve the photocatalytic
behavior of ZIF-8 [160]. Semiconductors can be used to produce
clean energy, they can also remediate environment by water split-
ting, CO2 reduction and pollutant degradation, they are the earliest
as well as the most extensively studied heterogeneous photocata-
lysts [161]. The main semiconductors that combined with ZIF-8
can be divided into two categories, one is n-type, including TiO2,
ZnO, CdS, ZnS and so on, this type of semiconductors usually take
electrons as the major charge carrier to determine conductivity;
The other is p-type, which generally take holes as the major charge
carriers, such as PbS, Co3O4, Cu2O and NiO. Since single-component
photocatalysts are susceptible to rapid electron-hole recombina-
tion, the ZIF-8 composites containing semiconductor materials
bring together the excellent physical and chemical properties of
these two phases, thereby improving the photocatalytic efficiency.
For example, when ZIF-8 is compounded with a semiconductor, the
metal cluster of the semiconductor can maintain its semiconductor
characteristics, in addition, the porous structure of ZIF-8 helps the
metal cluster to exert the maximum photocatalysis effect
[162,163].
Chandra et al. encapsulated TiO2 nanotubes into ZIF-8 and
obtained TiO2@ZIF-8 composite, then applied it to dyes degrada-
tion under visible light [164]. During the synthesis process, ZIF-8
acted as a host to ensure the stability of TiO2 and prevent it from
condensing. It could be clearly seen from UV-DRS spectra that
the microenvironment of TiO2 NPs was obviously changed within
ZIF-8 cage, which reduced the band gap of TiO2 and simultaneously
increase its photocatalytic activity [165]. After 2 h of catalysis, the
color of the dye can be completely eliminated. The removal rate of
TiO2@ZIF-8 for methylene blue can reach 87.5%, which was about 3
times the degradation rate of TiO2 nanotubes alone, and the
removal rate of rhodamine B can reach 64.85%, which was
14.85% higher than ZIF-8. In Chandra’s experiment, compared to
ZIF-8 or TiO2, TiO2@ZIF-8 had high recycling rate, while the TiO2
nanotubes basically had little catalytic efficiency after second
degradation.

4. Factors affect the removal efficiency of ZIF-8 composite
The properties of the ZIF-8 composites are related to many fac-
tors, such as the influence of the synthesis processed, which have
effect on water stability, specific surface area, pore volume and
pore size, surface chargeability. And the influence of external envi-
ronment: temperature, molecular polarity, pH, initial concentra-
tion of pollutants, dosage of adsorbent, and ionic strength also
counts. In this chapter, these factors are concluded and discussed
in detailed.
4.1. Effect of reaction conditions
When prepare ZIF-8 monomer, core size, reaction solvents, pre-
cursor concentration and growth circles will influence its particle
size distribution, specific surface area and particle size, and then
affect the morphology and properties of the composite. Therefore,
during the preparation process of ZIF-8, finding the optimal syn-
thesis method can increase the removal efficiency of ZIF-8 compos-
ites to a certain extent. At the same time, the types, chemical
properties and radio of the materials combined with ZIF-8 also
affect the ability of composites.
4.1.1. Preparation conditions of ZIF-8 monomer
At present, the synthesis methods of ZIF-8 mainly include
solvothermal method, stirring method, microwave method, vapor
phase method and so on. Among them, the solvothermal method
is one of the common synthetic methods, it has the advantages
of simple equipment and relatively perfect crystal growth, but its
high temperature and long-term preparation conditions still
restrict its practical application. In contrast, the stirring method
has become one of the most extensive synthesis methods by virtue
of its normal temperature, normal pressure, short time, and effi-
cient preparation conditions.
Solvents play a vital role in modifying the formation way of ZIF-
8 crystal and changing the morphology of crystal. The common sol-
vents are organic solvents (DMF, methanol, etc.) or water, different
solvent may lead to the different formation of ZIF-8 particles. In the
study of Peng’ group, they used LPE approach to deduce the forma-
tion situation of ZIF-8 shell on the SiO2 surface through using H2O
and MeOH as precursor solvents [106]. They found the crystal mor-
phology of ZIF-8 was different in these two solvents, and only in
the MeOH solvent can the uniform ZIF-8 layer could be formed
on the surface of SiO2. When put in H2O, ZIF-8 tends to form lay-
ered transient crystal phase.
The molar ratio of zinc nitrate to 2-methylimidazole, precursor
concentration will also affect the purity, specific surface area and
yield of ZIF-8. The addition of surfactant can effectively manipulate
and control the water system medium ZIF-8 shape and size. Mod-
ification of ZIF-8 with amino acids, thiols, etc. can increase the
specific surface area of ZIF-8 and improve its ability for pollutants
removal.
4.1.2. Preparation conditions of composites
In order to achieve the feasible control the performance of the
ZIF-8 compound, we should consider the following aspects: (i) it
is desirable to control the size, shape, composition and radio of
the incorporated materials, since such control can systematically
adjust the relevant properties of the them; (ii) protect the proper-
ties of incorporated materials (especially their optical and adsorp-
tion properties), meanwhile, keeping the separation of them to
minimize their agglomeration; (iii) choose a rational combination
method for functional materials and ZIF-8, as alternative routes

4.2.2. Initial concentration and adsorbent/photocatalyst dose
In general, the equilibrium time varies according to the concen-
tration of pollutants. Some researchers have concluded that a
higher concentration of pollutant will greatly inhibit the removal
efficiency [84]. This is because at lower concentrations, active sites
on the ZIF-8 composites are more available for pollutants. When
the concentration of pollutants is high, the pollutant will reduce
the path length of photons into the solution, thereby reducing
the penetration rate of light through the surface of the photocata-
lyst [191]. Akbari et al. assessed the effect of ZIF-8@PTA@AuNP
dosages and TC initial concentration on the removal of TC
(Fig. 10c) [86]. The increased dose of ZIF-8@PTA@Au promoted
the degradation rate of TC, and reaching a maximum at a dose of
0.6. They also found that the higher TC concentration was not con-
ducive to the progress of photocatalytic reaction (Fig. 10d), because
more TC molecules will be adsorbed on the surface of the photocat-
alyst, thereby preventing direct contact between active oxygen
species and TC molecules. It could also be seen from the curve of
irradiation time, the degradation efficiency of TC was greatly
improved in the first few minutes, and reached adsorption equilib-
rium after around ten minutes [192]. These theories are also
applied to the adsorption of dyes, the higher initial concentration
of dye can strengthen the driving force of the concentration gradi-
ent, while the lower concentration will shorten the time to reach
the adsorption equilibrium. In Wang et al.’s adsorption experiment,
they used ZIF-8@CS to remove CR, and found the increasing initial
concentration of CR caused a sharply decrease of removal effi-
ciency. With the increase of the adsorbent dosage, the removal effi-
ciency gradually increased by 40% [83]. Ehsan et al. also discussed
these two parameters when studied the adsorption performance of
the chitosan-g-PNVCL/ZIF-8 nanofibers. When increased the initial
concentrations of Cr(VI) and As(V), the reduction of porosity and
blockages of chitosan-g-PNVCL/ZIF-8 nanofibers pores resulted in
decreasing removal efficiency [139].
In general, there will exist an optimal dose of ZIF-8 composites,
and at that point, the adsorption efficiency is the highest. When the
amount is less than the optimal dose, the removal rate will gener-
ally increase with the amount of adsorbent increases, but the fur-
ther increase of adsorbent will cause particle interaction such as
aggregation or sedimentation [191]. The optimal dose also exists
when the ZIF-8 compound is used as a photocatalyst, before opti-
mal dose, available adsorption sites and photocatalytic activity will
be enhanced at larger amounts of composites. A higher photocata-
lyst amount will raise the turbidity of the solution, thereby elimi-
nating or reducing the influence of light. However, excessive
photocatalyst may precipitate and accumulate in solution and
result in the reduction of active species, light will also scatter
under this condition.

electron-poor GO material via p-p bond [205,206].

6. Conclusion and outlook
ZIF-8 overcomes the poor thermal stability and chemical stabil-
ity of MOF and becomes a successful material for sewage treat-
ment. More importantly, the high porosity and active sites of ZIF-
8 have led to the expanding applications of ZIF-8 based composites
in wastewater remediation and water regeneration. This review
gives a specific introduction to the common synthesis strategies
of ZIF-8 composites, and then discussed the application of compos-
ites for removing water pollutant as adsorbent and photocatalyst.
This study attempts to lay the theoretical foundation for the appli-
cation of ZIF-8 composite in practical wastewater treatment and
provides new ideas for constructing high-efficiency on MOF based
adsorbent and photocatalyst.
ZIF-8 composites not only remain the properties of any single
material but also exhibit enhanced performance because of the
synergistic function, they have inestimable application prospects
in industrial wastewater treatment. Based on the application status
of ZIF-8 composites for wastewater treatment in this review, we
are looking forward to the probable research directions of ZIF-8
composite in the future, as shown in the following aspects.
(i) Various synthesis methods of ZIF-8 composite are intro-
duced, such as encapsulating NPs into ZIF-8, loading NPs
on the surface of ZIF-8, or incorporating ZIF-8 with nanorods
(NPs/NRs), polymers, fibers and so on, to further develop and
expand the potential applications of ZIF-8. However, despite
the increasing reports on ZIF-8 composite materials, it is still
a great challenge to obtain an ideal ZIF-8 composite with
proper size, composition, dispersion properties, and spatial
distribution. The interaction between the matrix and the
reinforcement is the key to the successful preparation of
ZIF-8 composites. Thus, mechanism of interface reaction
between them needs continued exploration and explana-
tion. Moreover, researchers are seeking for innovative ways
to achieve simpler and more precise synthesis of composites,
such as sonochemical method, sol-gel method, microfluidic
method and so on. Meanwhile, with the purpose of maxi-
mizing the performance of a target composite, one or more
kinds of synthesis methods can be combined through slight
modification.
(ii) As we know, numerous ZIF-8 composites have been synthe-
sized, and a slight change in the synthesis condition may
cause the immense diverging properties of ZIF-8 composites.
Although the characterization technologies are constantly
improving and developing, the characterizations of the
