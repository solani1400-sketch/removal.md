3.8. Adsorption mechanism378
The adsorption mechanism of TC on SCG is complex, and the driving force of adsorption379
may be electrostatic or non electrostatic, in which non electrostatic effects include Van der380
Waals force, hydrophobic force, hydrogen bond and π-π interaction force (Moreno-Castilla381
et al., 2004). Combining the hydrophobic parameters of TC (Fig. 4 (a)) and the pHPZC of382
SCGs (Table 1-2), it was seen that the driving force of adsorption was electrical repulsion.383
However, with the effect of pH, the electrostatic interaction was not the main motivation for384
TC adsorption by SCGs.385
Besides, hydrophobic interaction is also not the main motivation of TC in SCGs386
adsorption. There existed a large amount of polar groups (oxygen-containing functional387
groups) on SCGs (Table 1-2), its surface was mainly hydrophilic groups (carboxylic group,388
lactonic group and phenolic group), so hydrophobic effect uneasily occurred (Chun et al.,389
2004). In addition, it can be seen from Fig. 4 (a) and Fig. 2 (b) that the amount of TC390
adsorbed on SCG did not agree with the trend of hydrophobic parameters with the change of391
pH, and hydrogen bonding might be the main thrust force for the adsorption of TC on SCGs.392
