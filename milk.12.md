A B S T R A C T
Biocompatible magnetic molecularly imprinted polymers (BMMIPs) were prepared with Zein for the first time,
and were used to enrich tetracycline compounds selectively. Innovative combination of BMMIPs and electro-
chemistry to obtain lower detection line to satisfy industrial detection demands. Using Zein as the crosslinking
agent, the polymers were synthesized on the surface of Fe3O4 particles. The scanning electron microscope,
transmission electron microscope and X-ray diffraction technologies were used to characterize BMMIPs. Through
optimization, BMMIPs attained large adsorption capacity (236.40 mg/g) with fast kinetics (40 min) and followed
the Langmuir isotherm and pseudo-second-order kinetic models. BMMIPs had good recognition ability, the se-
lective factors of oxytetracycline, chlortetracycline, doxycycline were 4.78, 4.23, and 3.39, respectively.
Excellent linearity was attained in the range of 0.025–500 μg/mL, with low detection limits and low quantitation
limits of 0.025 and 0.083 μg/mL. According to our exploring, BMMIPs was ideal materials for enrichment of
tetracycline in complex biological samples.
1. Introduction
Zein is the major storage protein of maize endosperm, and accounts
for approximately 80% of the total protein in corn. Zein is amphiphilic,
and more than 50% of the amino acid residues in Zein are hydrophobic,
which makes it soluble in aqueous ethanol solution (60–90%), but in-
soluble in water. Zein contains sulfur amino acids, and its protein
molecules are connected with strong disulfide bonds and hydrophobic
bonds (Dai et al., 2017; Wang, Su, Schulmerich, & Padua, 2013). The
characterization of Zein self-assembly can be evaluated by antisolvent
precipitation (ASP) and many other methods. Therefore, there have
been many reports described the application of this auto-polymeriza-
tion and biologically compatible material, such as Zein films that are
used as a carrier for sustained-release drugs (Chang, Wang, Hu, & Luo,
2017; Kashiri et al., 2017; Wang & Padua, 2012). Because Zein is a
biocompatible substance, it is highly advantageous to develop it for
using in medicine and the food industry (Corradini et al., 2014; Cui,
Qiu, Qiu, Wang, & Wei, 2015).
The molecular imprinting technique (MIT) was developed on the
basis of bionic science and simulation of antigens and antibodies
(Ashley et al., 2017). Molecularly imprinted polymer (MIP) contains a
three-dimensional cavity constructed by a template, and this substance
is widely used in optical fiber sensors, chromatographic separation,
biofilm sensors, and drug release. MIP has the ability to remember
specific shapes and sizes of specific analyses or structure-related com-
pounds (Wang, Zhang, Song, Liu, & Wang, 2017; Xie et al., 2017). Due
to its advantages of simple preparation, excellent stability, high se-
lectivity, and low cost, MIP is often used as an adsorbent for solid phase

Tetracycline (TC) drugs were first discovered in the 1940s and were
used as an effective bacteriostatic agent and animal fungicide. Number
of tetracycline resistance genes encoding is numerous, to spread easily
between bacteria (Guarddon et al., 2011). The abuse of these drugs
resulted in side effects such as tetracycline teeth and bacterial re-
sistance, which were very harmful to human health. Therefore, it was
necessary to develop some economical and highly selective enrichment
methods to monitor the antimicrobial residues (Ma, Li, Chen, Sun, &
Yan, 2016). There are many methods to determine TC residues, such as
capillary electrophoresis (CE), flow injection analysis (FIA) (Ibarra,
Rodriguez, Miranda, Vega, & Barrado, 2011; Rodriguez, Espinosa,
Aguilar-Arteaga, Ibarra, & Miranda, 2010), high performance liquid
chromatography (HPLC), mass spectrometry (MS), and infrared (IR)
spectroscopy (Martins et al., 2014; Zhu et al., 2016). However, the
impurities in the actual samples could affect the accuracy of the
method, and result in relatively high limits of detection. It is necessary
to first remove impurities, so that tetracycline remains can be pre-
served, which requires the pretreatment of samples using methods such
as solid-phase extraction or liquid–liquid extraction. Solid-phase ex-
traction is a commonly used method (Sánchez-Polo, Velo-Gala, López-
Peñalver, & Rivera-Utrilla, 2015). Accordingly, MIPs can specifically
extract target components from complex samples. The BMMIPs may
help solve these difficulties.
Herein, we reported the synthesis of Zein based on magnetic mo-
lecular imprinting using tetracycline as a template, and then described
the results of various characterizations of BMMIPs. The selectivity of
BMMIPs for different tetracycline drugs was investigated. Combined
with an electrochemical detection (ECD) method, BMMIPs has been
innovated for industrial development. In this study, by implementing
various aspects of green chemistry for the synthesis of BMMIPs, we
demonstrated that our method is highly applicable and sensitive, which
could be a new option for identification applications for complex bio-
logical samples.

2.3. Synthesis of imprinted and non-imprinted nanomaterial
2.3.1. Prepare Fe3O4 nanoparticles
Nanoparticles of Fe3O4 magnetic are prepared by solvent heating
method (Zhang, Kuang, Zhang, Yang, & Lu, 2013). In general, 1.35 g of
FeCl3·6H2O was added to 40 mL of ethylene glycol, and then it was
completely dissolved in magnetic stirring to become a clear and
transparent solution at room temperature. Thereafter, 3.1 g of sodium
acetate anhydrous and 0.4 g of sodium citrate were added into the
solution. The mixtures were fully stirred under 150 °C, until formed
partial black uniform solution, then was transferred to a Teflon-lined
stainless-steel autoclave. The autoclave was supported at 200 °C for 8 h.
Finally, the materials were washed by anhydrous ethanol and ultrapure
water, then the materials were dried in the vacuum drying chamber at
50 °C for 8 h.
2.3.2. Prepare BMMIPs and BMNIPs
Magnetic molecularly imprinted nano-materials were synthesized
using MAA as functional monomer and Zein as crosslinking agent. TC
(0.06 g), 75% ethanol–water (50 mL) and MAA (125 μL) were mixed in
a florence flask and pre-aggregated for 12 h. Then Fe3O4 (50 mg), Zein
(250 mg) were added for continuous oscillation for 2 h. After the os-
cillation was completed, the mixture was drawn out with a magnet, and
then washed with 75% ethanol for three times, then by water (30 mL)
for three times. Finally, it was put into a shaking table for one night to
make it self-polymerize. The material was air dried after self-poly-
merization was completed, and then Methanol-HAc (9:1, v/v) was
added as eluent to remove the TC template. The products were eluted
repeatedly until no adsorption was observed by HPLC at 350 nm. The
nano-materials were collected by a magnetic field and dried at 45 °C.
For comparison, biocompatible surface magnetic non-molecular im-
printed polymers (BMNIPs) were synthesized by the same method
without adding TC. The synthetic routine of BMMIPs is shown in Fig. 1.

3. Results and discussion
3.1. BMMIP preparation
3.1.1. The optimization of conditions
As a cross-linker, Zein forms a film on the surface of a magnetic
material with a change in solvent polarity. Through the analysis of the
molecular structure of Zein, it was found that amine and imine subunits
were on the main chain form hydrogen bonds. Because Zein easily
forms sticky clumps in water, it was used as crosslinking agent.
To investigate the solubleness of Zein, we dissolved Zein in etha-
nol–water at different proportions, and found that 75% ethanol had the
best solubleness for Zein.
3.1.2. The molar ratio of template molecule and methacrylic acid (MAA)
Functional monomers play a crucial role in the process of BMMIPs
formation. They can form chemical interactions with template mole-
cules and help to establish the imprinting site. The molar ratio of
template molecule (TC) and MAA (at 1:9, 1:10, 1:11, and 1:12) was
investigated, and the molar ratio at 1:11 was found to be the most
optimal, according to the morphological features.
3.2. BMMIP characterization
3.2.1. The TEM and SEM analysis of Fe3O4 NPs, BMMIPs, and BMNIPs
The morphological characteristics of Fe3O4 NPs, BMNIPs, and
BMMIPs were observed by scanning electron microscopy (SEM) (Fig. 2).
The Fe3O4 NPs are spherical, approximately 380 nm in diameter, and
are highly monodispersed (Fig. 2A). The size of the BMMIPs and
BMNIPs were uniform, with a diameter between 400 and 420 nm
(Fig. 2B and S3). In addition, the BMMIPs and BMNIPs TEM micro-
graphs were shown in Fig. 2C and E. Obviously, in the small field of
view, the BMMIPs (Fig. 2D) was larger in size than the BMNIPs
(Fig. 2F), and the outer layer was partially transparent, that is to say the
outer layer of BMMIPs was wrapped by Zein. Thus, BMMIPs was pre-
pared with a core–shell structure.

3.2.3. TGA analysis
As the results shown in Fig. 3B, TGA analysis was used to further
study the BMMIP nanoparticles. TGA curve of BMMIPs showed two
stages of weight loss in the 25–800 °C range. The first weight loss stage
was due to the disappearance of the unbound water and the structural
water molecules from 25 °C to 120 °C. The second weight loss stage of
12.5% within the 120 °C and 400 °C range was due to the thermal
decomposition and the pyrolyzation of organic solvents. Overall, al-
though the weight loss was only 17.5%, the weight loss rate in the
second stage was faster than that in the first stage, so BMMIPs was
considered to have good thermal stability below 120 °C.
3.2.4. XRD characterization
The X-ray diffraction patterns for BMMIPs and Fe3O4 were shown in
Fig. 3C. In 2θ = 20–70°, found in 2θ = 30.45°, 35.66°, 43.17°, 53.39°,
57.04°, and 62.87° in the six characteristics of the Fe3O4 diffraction

3.2.6. Magnetic property investigation
To test the magnetic properties of BMMIPs, a permanent magnet
was placed near a glass bottle containing a solution of water and
BMMIPs (Fig. 3D). The results showed that the BMMIPs could be se-
parated from the aqueous solution relatively quickly, within 10 s. When
the external magnetic field was removed, the magnetic reaction dis-
appeared. After applying a coating of Zein polymer, no significant
change in the magnetic response of the BMMIPs was observed. This
phenomenon was due to the fact that the Zein coating effectively
shielded magnetite, but had no obvious negative effects on magnetic
function. Therefore, the sample can be quickly separated by conven-
tional magnets in a short time.
