A B S T R A C T
A novel cellulose composite (denoted as PEI@MMA-1) with porous interconnected structure was prepared by
adsorbing methyl cellulose (MC) onto microcrystalline cellulose (MCC) and cross-linking polyethyleneimine
(PEI) with MCC by the action of epichlorohydrin, which had the excellent adsorption property, wettability and
elasticity. The performances of PEI@MMA-1 composite for removing tetracycline (TC), Cu
2+ 
and coexistent
pollutant (TC and Cu
2+ 
mixture) were systematically explored. For single TC or Cu
2+ 
contaminant, the maximum
adsorption capacities were 75.53 and 562.23 mg/g at 30 
◦
C, respectively, while in the dual contaminant system,
they would form complexes and Cu
2+ 
could play a “bridge” role to remarkably promote the adsorption of TC
with the maximum adsorption capacities of 281.66 and 253.58 mg/g for TC and Cu
2+
. In addition, the adsorption
kinetics, isotherms and adsorption mechanisms of single-pollutant and dual-pollutant systems have been thor-
oughly investigated. Theoretical calculations indicated that the amide group of TC molecule with the assistance
of Cu
2+ 
interacted with the hydroxyl group of PEI@MMA-1 composite to enhance the TC adsorption capacity.
Cycle regeneration and fixed bed column experiments revealed that the PEI@MMA-1 possessed the excellent
stability and utility. Current PEI@MMA-1 cellulose composite exhibited a promising application for remediation
of heavy metals and antibiotics coexistence wastewater.
1. Introduction
Recently, some concerns have been expressed about coexisting heavy
metals and antibiotics in water sources. The animal husbandry industry
inevitably uses animal pharmaceuticals and additives for disease pre-
vention and growth promoters that contribute to animal development,
and as a result, it is frequently found that their discharged wastewater
contains both heavy metals and antibiotics (Saravanan et al., 2021).
Since antibiotics and heavy metal ions can create more robust com-
plexes, they can survive in the environment for a long time. Moreover,
the studies have shown that the combined toxicity of these complexes is
usually higher than their individual toxicity (Guo et al., 2018). In
addition, because antibiotics and heavy metals have very different
chemical properties, their transformation pathways are also very
different, and the interaction between them can greatly affect the
treatment efficiency (Min et al., 2018). Therefore, it is important to
study their environmental behavior, and create efficient techniques for
simultaneous removing antibiotics and heavy metals.
Adsorption is an effective approach for removing heavy metal ions or
antibiotic pollutants due to its economic and convenient operation
(Novikau & Lujaniene, 2022). The key point is in designing and con-
structing the highly efficient adsorbents. At present, activated carbon
(Jang et al., 2018), polymer resins (Ling et al., 2013), graphene oxide
(Feng et al., 2022), metal oxides (Kim et al., 2021) and others have been
employed to remove antibiotic and heavy metal ions simultaneously.
Perovskite (K2FeO4) and wheat straw have been used as precursors by
Yan et al. (2022) to prepare iron-modified biochar, which has the
maximal adsorption capacities of 46.85 mg/L for Cu
2+ 
and 45.43 mg/L
for sulfadiazine (SDZ), respectively. The hydrothermal approach has
been doped by Feng et al. (2022) to fabricate a three-dimensional g-
C3N4/graphene oxide (GO) hydrogel for the simultaneous adsorption of
tetracycline (TC) and copper ions (Cu
2+
). The adsorption capacity of
Cu
2+ 
is about 2.5 mmol/g and that of TC is round 3.0 mmol/g. Although
these adsorbent materials can remove both antibiotics and heavy metals,

Cellulose aerogels with porous architectures and a lot of functional
groups show the outstanding adsorption properties towards numerous
organic contaminants and heavy metals. Owing to their abundance of
oxygen-containing functional groups, they can be easily modified to
significantly improve the potential for water treatment (Gao et al.,
2021). However, the existing cellulose aerogels still face some inherent
drawbacks, such as weak stability, single functional group, and low
mechanical strength, which prevents them from meeting the re-
quirements for high efficacy and cost-effective wastewater treatment (Li
et al., 2022; Lma et al., 2021; Tang et al., 2020). To address these
drawbacks, aerogel modification techniques have been proposed: (1)
Introducing polymers such as proteins, chitosan (CS), methyl cellulose
(MC) and corn starch on the surface of cellulose to form composites by
biopolymer adsorption phenomena, which can enhance the mechanical
properties of the composites (Ahsan et al., 2019; Kedzior et al., 2017);
(2) Using particular functional groups to modify the surfaces of cellulose
aerogels. Cellulose treated with amine functional groups (-NH2, -NH-)
can improve antibiotic and heavy metal adsorption by coordination. As
a green amine graft modifier, polyethyleneimine (PEI) can not only
improve the adsorption capabilities, but also construct a three-
dimensional network structure to increase the aerogels’ mechanical
elasticity (Chen et al., 2022; Li et al., 2018; Wang, Li, Kong, et al., 2022).
Inspired by the above, we design and construct an amino-
functionalized cellulose composite with honeycomb porous structure
to efficiently remove both TC and Cu
2+ 
from water. Although there are
some reports on the removal of TC or Cu
2+ 
by cellulose aerogels (Hong
et al., 2021; Ma et al., 2021; Mo et al., 2022; Wei et al., 2018), they
usually act on a single contaminant (TC or Cu
2+
). To the best of our
knowledge, this work is the first account of the removal of antibiotic and
heavy metal by cellulose composite simultaneously. The findings show
that the addition of MC improves the compressive resistance of com-
posite; the PEI macromolecular enhances the mechanical strength,
meanwhile the abundant amino functional groups have a good ability to
fixate metal ions. The impacts of pH value, contact time, starting con-
centration and coexisting ions on the adsorption performance of TC or/
and Cu
2+ 
are systematically investigated. The potential for the cellulose
composite in practical applications is confirmed using fixed-bed
adsorption column studies. The FTIR, XPS and density functional the-
ory (DFT) calculation are applied to disclose the adsorption mechanism.
2. Materials and methods
2.1. Materials
Microcrystalline cellulose (MCC, analytical grade, (C6H10O5)n, n =
220, molecular weight ca. 35,600) and polyethyleneimine (PEI,
analytical grade, 50 wt%, (CH2CH2NH)n, molecular weight 70,000)
were obtained from Shanghai Aladdin Reagent Co. Ltd. Methyl cellulose
(MC, analytical grade, degree of substitution 1.7) was purchased from
Shanghai Macklin Biochemical Technology Co. Ltd. Methyl cellulose
specification sheet and molecular weight (ca. 1600000, determined by
gel permeation chromatography test) were provided in Fig. S1.
Epichlorohydrin (ECH, C3H5ClO), ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid
disodium salt (EDTA-2Na), sodium hydroxide, urea and other chemical
reagents were purchased from Shanghai Sinopharm Chemical Reagent
Co. Ltd. Analytical grade reagents were employed throughout the
experiment without further purification. The studies were conducted
with deionized water.
2.2. Preparation of cellulose composite
The typical preparation procedures of cellulose composite are as
follows: A solution of NaOH (3.43 g), urea (5.9 g) and water (39 g)
(7:12:81, w/w/w) was first pre-cooled at   18 
◦
C for 1 h. Then, to obtain
a 2 wt% MCC mixture, 1.0 g of MCC was added in this pre-cooled so-
lution and stirred magnetically for 2 h in a 5 
◦
C cold-water bath until the
MCC was entirely dissolved. Meanwhile, 1.0 g MC was dispersed in 49 g
water and agitated for 2 h at room temperature to prepare 2 wt% MC
solution. The MCC mixture and MC solution were mixed thoroughly for
1 h at room temperature to create MCC-MC composite. Afterword, in the
aforementioned composite, 0.1, 0.5, 1.0 and 2.0 g PEI were added and
magnetically agitated at room temperature for 30 min. Next, 0.5 g ECH
was added and magnetically stirred at room temperature for an addi-
tional 4 h. The cross-linking polymerization reaction was fully occurred
to form the cellulose composite gel. To remove the unreacted reagents
(NaOH and urea) and create cellulose composite hydrogel, the com-
posite gel was introduced into a dialysis bag (MW = 3500) for dialysis
treatment until the dialysis solution was neutral. Following, the cellu-
lose composite hydrogel was kept at   18 
◦
C for 12 h in a refrigerator.
Finally, the hydrogel was subjected to freeze-drying treatment at   50 
◦
C
for 48 h to obtain amino-functionalized cellulose composite. According
to the addition amounts of PEI (0.1, 0.5, 1.0 and 2.0 g), the as-prepared
cellulose composites were labeled as PEI@MMA-0.1, PEI@MMA-0.5,
PEI@MMA-1 and PEI@MMA-2, respectively. Scheme 1 illustrates the
cellulose composite synthesis procedure. For comparison, cellulose
composite material without PEI (labeled as MMA), and the microcrys-
talline cellulose composite excluding MC and PEI (labeled as MA) were
also prepared by the same method.
2.3. Adsorption experiments
Batch adsorption experiments were performed on a thermostatic
oscillator at 30 
◦
C with a 150 rpm/min oscillation frequency. Appro-
priate amounts of adsorbent were added into 50 mL of solution con-
taining TC or/and Cu
2+
. To determine the optimal environmental
conditions for the adsorption, the influence of pH value on the adsorp-
tion performance was explored by adjusting the solution pH value using
0.1 M NaOH or 0.1 M H2SO4 solutions; the effect of ionic strength on the
adsorption process was also explored by adding NaCl in the solution. The
adsorption kinetic characteristics were examined by adding 0.4 g/L of
adsorbent into different TC concentration solutions (20, 50 and 100 mg/
L) or Cu
2+ 
solutions (50, 100 and 150 mg/L) and collecting solution
samples at fixed time intervals (0, 2, 5, 10, 20, 30, 60, 90, 120, 150, 180,
240 and 360 min). The adsorption isotherm experiments were explored
by adding 0.4 g/L of adsorbent in different concentrations of TC (20 to
100 mg/L) or Cu
2+ 
(20 to 200 mg/L) solutions at different temperatures
(30, 40 and 50 
◦
C) and shaking for 12 h to reach equilibrium. Co-
adsorption experiments were performed in a mixed reaction system
(binary pollutant system) containing TC (50 mg/L) and Cu
2+ 
(50 mg/L).
To exclude the effect of TC complexation with Cu
2+
, the mixed solution
was left for 2 h before adding the adsorbent. After 2 h resting, the
concentrations of TC and Cu
2+ 
in the solution were 46.28 mg/L and
48.68 mg/L, respectively. In order to investigate the interaction between
TC and Cu
2+ 
in the binary system, the concentration of Cu
2+ 
was
changed while the concentration of TC remained constant, and vice
versa. To investigate the removal effect of TC or/and Cu
2+ 
in different
water systems, deionized water, tap water, river water (collected from
Yangtze River water) and industrial wastewater (collected from a local
factory wastewater) were used as background solutions, Table S1 lists
the compositional parameters of tap water and Yangtze River water

rpm for 5 min, filtered through a 0.45 μm filter membrane, and the
concentrations of TC and Cu
2+ 
solutions were measured at 356 nm using

2.4. Regeneration and reusability of cellulose composite
Six cycles of regeneration tests were performed using 0.2 M EDTA-
2Na and HCl solutions as resolving agents for Cu
2+ 
and TC in the
adsorbed cellulose composites, respectively. In particular, the cellulose
composite was repeatedly pressed 20 times after immersing it in the
resolving agent to speed up the resolution process. For the succeeding
cycle regeneration test, the resolved cellulose composite was freeze-
dried after being rinsed with deionized water.
2.5. Column adsorption experiments
To estimate the continuous adsorption property of cellulose com-
posites (PEI@MMA-1) for TC or/and Cu
2+
, a cylindrical filled column
with a diameter of 30.0 mm and a length of 200.0 mm was fabricated,
and cellulose composites with a diameter of 30 ± 1.0 mm and a length of
80 ± 5.0 mm were prepared in a mold to accurately fill the column
reactor. Specifically, after placing the as-prepared cellulose composite
into the filled column, a peristaltic pump (BT100-2 J) was used to flow
10 mg/L of TC or/and Cu
2+ 
solution into the column from the bottom up
at a flow rate of 20 mL/min. The liquid sample was collected from the
outlet port every 5 min.
2.6. Density functional theory (DFT) calculations
To understand the interaction between adsorbate (TC and Cu
2+
) and
adsorbent (PEI@MMA-1), theoretical calculations were performed using
the Gaussian 16 suite of programs. Since the structure of PEI@MMA-1
was very large and its initial structure needed to be simplified when
performing DFT calculations, and its monomeric cross-linked structure
was chosen as the calculation model. The detailed calculation method
was provided in Text S2.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Characterization of cellulose composites
As shown in Fig. 1a and Fig. 1d, the shape of petals does not change
significantly when MMA and PEI@MMA-1 are placed on the soft petals,
illustrating the ultra-lightness of two cellulose composites, which allows
them to float on the surface of water and facilitate subsequent adsorp-
tion recycling (Zhang et al., 2022). Fig. 1b and Fig. 1c display the field
emission scanning electron microscope (FESEM) images of MMA, from
which it is clear that MMA presents a wrinkled lamellar hierarchical
stacking structure, and the surface of the layers is relatively rough. As a
comparison, the FESEM image of MA is shown in Fig. S2, where MA
presents an irregular lamellar structure. Fig. 1e and Fig. 1f exhibit the
morphology of PEI@MMA-1, and it can be seen that it has a porous,
honeycomb-like skeletal structure as a result of cross-linking between
PEI and MCC, which creates a three-dimensional hierarchical structure.
This unusual honeycomb-shaped porous structure can offer mass
transfer channels for water molecule penetration and ion diffusion in
water treatment (Wang et al., 2021). In addition, the three-dimensional
interconnected porous structure of PEI@MMA-1 can more effectively
counteract the external stresses during compression to impart superior
compression resilience compared with the laminar structure of MMA.
The elemental mapping images of PEI@MMA-1 (Fig. 1g-1j) depict the
uniform distribution of elements (C, N and O) on its internal skeleton,
reflecting that PEI@MMA-1 is rich in internal nitrogen- and oxygen-
containing functional groups, which offers a significant number of
active sites for the adsorption process (Hong et al., 2021).
The pore structure and pore size distribution of PEI@MMA-1 are
investigated by mercury pressure test. As shown in Fig. S3a, in the first
stage, with the increase of mercury pressure, mercury rapidly accumu-
lates and fills into the pore volume between the voids of cellulose
composite. In the second stage, under high pressure, mercury fills into

3.2.1. Effect of environmental conditions
The PEI addition amount has an impact on the quantity of nitrogen-
containing functional group adsorption sites in the PEI-MMA cellulose
composite, in turn affecting their adsorption performance. The adsorp-
tion capacities of cellulose composites with the different PEI addition
amounts for TC or Cu
2+ 
are presented in Fig. 4a. The cellulose com-
posite’s ability to adsorb TC or Cu
2+ 
gradually becomes more effective
as PEI content is increased, and the PEI@MMA-1 sample has the
maximum adsorption capacity for Cu
2+ 
and the better adsorption ca-
pacity for TC (slightly lower than PEI-MMA-0.5 sample). The adsorption
capacity decreases when the PEI addition amount is above a certain level
(e.g., PEI@MMA-2 sample). This may be because too much PEI causes
the excessive cross-linking, which ruins the uniformity of honeycomb
structure inside the PEI-MMA cellulose composite and reduces the
amount of effective adsorption sites (Mo et al., 2021). Accordingly, the
PEI@MMA-1 sample is chosen as the ideal sample for the subsequent
studies.
The solution pH value determines the form of adsorbate in water and
the surface charge of adsorbent, which will significantly affect the
adsorption performance of adsorbent, and thus exploring the ideal so-
lution pH value is essential. It is worth noting that at pH > 6.0, Cu
2+ 
is
present as a precipitate of Cu(OH)2, and its removal cannot be attributed
to the action of adsorbent at this time (Zhao, Chen, et al., 2022).
Therefore, the initial solution pH value for Cu
2+ 
adsorption is set to
2.0–6.0. The adsorption capacity of PEI@MMA-1 for Cu
2+ 
increases
gradually with an increase in the initial pH value, as shown in Fig. 4c,
and reaches its maximum capacity at pH = 6.0. This is mainly due to the
decrease of H
+ 
in the solution, which weakens the electrostatic repulsion
of Cu
2+ 
on the cellulose composite surface (Mo et al., 2019). When the
solution pH value fluctuates from 3.0 to 11.0, the adsorption capacity of
PEI@MMA-1 for TC shows a trend of increasing and subsequently
dropping, with a better adsorption capacity at pH value of 5.0–7.0
(Fig. 4d). This is mostly due to the fact that TC molecules exist in
positively charged form (TC
+
) at pH < 3.3. Since the zero-charge point
of PEI@MMA-1 is 7.16 (Fig. 4b), the surface of PEI@MMA-1 is currently
positively charged. As a result, the electrostatic repulsion causes the
decrease of adsorption capacity (Qin et al., 2022). When 3.3 < pH <
7.68, TC molecules are present in neutral form (TC
±
) and electrostatic
interaction has a negligible impact on the adsorption performance.
Similar to this, when pH > 7.68, TC is present in the anionic form (TC
 
and TC
2  
), and this situation causes a reduction in the adsorption ca-
pacity due to electrostatic repulsion with the negatively charged
PEI@MMA-1 on the surface (Yan et al., 2020). Moreover, the positive pH
changes in the solution after Cu
2+ 
adsorption (Fig. S5a) is caused by the
large removal of Cu
2+
, while the pH change in the solution after TC
adsorption is relatively small (Fig. S5b), primarily because the presence
of TC molecules has little impact on the solution pH value (Dai et al.,
2020). From the above experimental results, it is clear that 6.0 is the
ideal pH for TC or Cu
2+ 
adsorption.

2+

and at the equilibrium of water absorption and swelling, the absorbed
masses of both reach 13.62 g and 15.31 g, respectively. After 20 cycles of
the repeated squeezing, water absorption and swelling, the absorbed
masses of MMA and PEI@MMA-1 still respectively reach 10.84 g and
12.34 g at the equilibrium of water absorption and swelling, indicating
that the internal structures of these cellulose composites are not severely
damaged during repeated squeezing. Meanwhile, the PEI@MMA-1 cel-
lulose composite exhibits the superior water stability than that of MMA
in view of the mass loss rate. Fig. 12g depicts the wet resilience mech-
anism of PEI@MMA-1 when it is pressurized under water. Because its
three-dimensional interconnected network porous structure is deformed
rather than collapsed after adsorbing water, it can return to its initial
size and recover the whole structure (Mo et al., 2022). Regarding the
excellent compression resilience, PEI@MMA-1 facilitates its recovery
and provides an important basis for its application in practical waste-
water treatment.
The recycling performance of adsorbent is a part that cannot be
neglected in practical applications (Zhao, Ding, et al., 2022). Fig. S13a
shows that the removal rate of Cu
2+ 
can still maintain above 91 % even
after six cycles. In the dual contaminants system (Fig. S13b), the removal
rate of TC can reach 92 % at the first time due to the promotion effect by
Cu
2+
, and even after six cycles, the removal rates of TC and Cu
2+ 
remain
above 80 %. The removal rates of TC and Cu
2+ 
gradually decline with
the number of cycles, which may be explained by the fact that the stable
complexes formed by Cu
2+ 
and (TC + Cu
2+ 
mixture) with PEI@MMA-1
cannot be completely removed, thus occupying some active sites of
PEI@MMA-1 and blocking its continued adsorption (Wang et al., 2021).
Despite this, PEI@MMA-1 adsorbent can be still considered to possess
the high removal efficiency and reusability.
4. Conclusions
In summary, an amino-functionalized cellulose composite
(PEI@MMA-1) with the excellent adsorption performance and excellent
underwater resilience was successfully prepared and applied for the
adsorption of TC or/and Cu
2+ 
contaminants. For the single contaminant
system (TC or Cu
2+
), the maximum adsorption capacities of PEI@MMA-
1 for TC and Cu
2+ 
were 75.53 mg/g and 562.24 mg/g at 30 
◦
C,
respectively, and the adsorption isotherm results indicated that the
adsorption of TC was more suitable for Freundlich isotherm model and
the adsorption of Cu
2+ 
was more suitable for Langmuir isotherm model.
For the dual contaminant system (TC + Cu
2+ 
mixture), the maximum
adsorption capacities of TC and Cu
2+ 
were 281.66 mg/g and 253.58 mg/
g at 30 
◦
C, respectively, and both were more suitable for the Langmuir
isotherm model. In addition, adsorption kinetic and thermodynamic
studies confirmed that the adsorption processes of TC, Cu
2+ 
and TC +
Cu
2+ 
all satisfied the chemisorption and spontaneous nature. The
adsorption of TC mainly proceeded through pore filling, π-π and n-π EDA
interactions, hydrogen bonding and electrostatic interactions; the
adsorption mechanism of Cu
2+ 
mainly involved pore filling, surface
complexation and electrostatic interactions; while the adsorption of TC
+ Cu
2+ 
mixtures involved, in addition to the mechanism of single
contamination, the formation of metal-organic complexes between TC
and Cu
2+ 
that bound to the nitrogen-containing and oxygen-containing
functional groups of PEI@MMA-1 through the “bridge” action of Cu
2+
.
The fixed-bed column and recycling experiments confirmed the excel-
lent utility of PEI@MMA-1 cellulose composite. The study provides a
new insight to construct the cellulose composite material for the
removal of heavy metal and antibiotic pollution in the wastewater.
