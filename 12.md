In the present study, novel ionic liquid-impregnated chitosan hydrogel beads (CS-TCMA) were fabricated via the
reaction of tricaprylmethylammonium chloride (TCMA, Aliquat-336) with the chitosan's amino groups. They
were used for the fast adsorption of Tetracycline (TC), as a pharmaceutical compound model, from aqueous so-
lution. It was found that the impregnation of TCMA greatly improved the adsorption behaviour of chitosan to-
ward TC. The optimum adsorbent was determined to be 3 mg/ L in a wide pH range of 5–11. It was a fast
process, with a 90% removal efficiency in b45 min. The adsorption kinetic of TC on the CS-TCMA was well de-
scribed by the pseudo-first-order model and intra-particle diffusion model. The adsorption also obeyed the Lang-
muir adsorption isotherm model and the maximum adsorption capacity obtained was 22.42 mg/g at 45 °C. The
thermodynamic study also revealed the endothermic nature of the process. The adsorption mechanism was
also studied.

1. Introduction
Antibiotics are among the most used pharmaceutical chemicals in
human treatment, the agricultural industry and also as additives in an-
imal feed [1]. Despite excessive increase in their consumption, most of
them are excreted to the environment by humans or animals through
urine and stool [2], and then can spread to agricultural fertilisers. They
can also enter the waste systems of medical centres and pharmaceutical
companies and threaten the health of living organisms [3]. They have
been discovered in soil, ground water, surface water and even drinking
water [4], are classified as a severe cause of water and soil contamina-
tion and their removal has received great attention as an important
worldwide issue [5,6].
Tetracycline (TC) is the second most produced and used antibiotic in
the world, due to its cheapness and high antimicrobial activity [7,8]. This
has caused its accumulation in the environment and the creation of fun-
damental problems for the aquatic environment, especially in aquatic
systems [7,8], so several attempts have been made to find an efficient
and applicable approach for the removal of TC-carrying water and
wastewater. In the last decade, various chemical and physical methods
have been employed to this end, including photocatalysis [9,10], chem-
ical oxidation [11], bio-degradation [12], extraction [13], adsorption
[14,15] and membrane technology [16]. Adsorption technology has
been applied to TC removal from aqueous solutions, as an efficient ap-
proach with the advantages of ease of use, low cost and high efficiency
[17]. In this regard, different adsorbents have been studied, such as ac-
tivated carbon [18], biochar [19], carbon nanotubes [20], clays and min-
erals [2,7], graphene oxide [21], magnetite [22], chitosan [23], etc.
The environmental issues are motivating researchers to focus on
green materials and sustainable technologies. Recently, ionic liquids
(ILs) have attracted great attention in a wide range of applications. ILs
are organic salts with marvellous properties, including low melting
point (below 100 °C) and high viscosity, chemical stability, conductivity,
acidity, polarity and hydrophobicity [24], depending on the nature of
their cationic and anionic species [25]. These characteristics have en-
couraged the use of ILs in wide range of applications, including green
solvents, catalysis and agents [26,27]. They have been considered popu-
lar alternatives in separation and purification technologies, such as liq-
uid and solid phase extraction and microextraction [28,29], liquid-
liquid extraction [30] and so on. They are usually hydrophilic and,
when directly used as anion exchanger in water treatment, may cause
secondary pollution. Recently, they have been employed to the modifi-
cation of different adsorbents to improve their adsorption performance
toward organic and inorganic pollutants from aqueous solution [31].

Chitosan ((1 → 4)-2-amino-2-deoxy-β-D-glucan), as a second abun-
dant biopolymer, is a low-cost natural polysaccharide, which has been
used as an efficient sorbent for the removal of different water pollutants,
due to its amino and hydroxyl groups [32–37]. The impregnation of chi-
tosan derivatives with ILs has been introduced as a novel approach to
improve their adsorption characteristics [31,38].
In the present study, the chitosan beads were impregnated with
tricaprylmethylammonium chloride (TCMA), in order to enhance their
adsorption toward TC from aqueous solution. TCMA, known as an ion-
pairing reagent, is a quaternary ammonium IL [39], which has been ef-
fectively used for the fabrication of ion-selective membrane electrodes
[40] as well as in the removal of organic and inorganic compounds
from wastewater or the recovery of acids/acid salts [41–43]. The effect
of different parameters on the adsorption efficiency, and also the ad-
sorption kinetic, isotherm and thermodynamic were investigated
comprehensively.

2.2. Preparation of TCMA-impregnated chitosan hydrogel beads (CS-TCMA)
The CS-TCMA was fabricated using the method reported in the
previous study [44]. In summary, TCMA solution containing 0.1 mL
of ethanol was dissolved in the solution with 0.5 g of chitosan in
acetic acid (1% v/v) and stirred for 3 h. The solution was added to
NaOH (1 M) drop by drop and stirred for 2 h to harden. The hydrogel
beads (CS-TCMA) obtained were washed several times with water
and dried at room temperature. The synthesis procedure is shown

3.1. Adsorption study
The removal of TC, through the adsorption onto the surface of CS-
TCMA beads was investigated. The preliminary results showed that
very well the adsorption of TC onto CS-TCMA, with the addition of
0.1 mL of TCMA greatly enhancing the adsorption efficiency of the hy-
drogel beads toward TC from ~9.8% to ~66% (see Fig. 3a). However,
the great addition of TCMA (i.e. 1 mL) can cause a disturbance in the ad-
sorption performance, which can be due to the covering and reduction
of adsorption sites. 0.1 mL was chosen as the amount of TCMA in the
preparation of hydrogel CS beads for further studies.

increases from 23% to 90% by increasing the CS-TCMA amount from 0.25
to 3 mg/L, due to the presence of a larger available surface area and

The adsorption of species form the liquid phase is a multi-stage pro-
cess involving the following steps: (i) bulk diffusion, (ii) film diffusion,
(iii) intra-particle diffusion, and (iv) chemical reaction with the active
sites on the surface of adsorbent [45]. The intra-particle diffusion
model was also applied to understand the diffusion mechanism of TC
molecules on/in the CS-TCMA hydrogel beads (see Table 1) [53]. As
shown in Fig. 5b, the evaluation of experimental data indicates that
the adsorption of TC onto CS-TCMA occurs in three different stages of
bulk, film and intra-particle diffusions. The Kid2 value is larger than
Kid1 and kid3, demonstrating that the rate of TC adsorption onto the sur-
face of CS-TCMA is controlled by the film penetration stage or penetra-
tion of the intrinsic material.

3.3. Desorption and reusability studies
The adsorbent was easily regenerated using H2SO4 solution (0.01 M)
and its reusability was studied by adsorption-desorption cycles, which
were repeated for four times. As can be seen in Fig. 7a, the TC adsorption
efficiency decreased ~10% after the 4th reuse cycle and the structure of
sorbent was unchanged, indicating the stability of sorbent (see Fig. 7b).

4. Conclusions
In the present work, the tricaprylmethylammonium chloride im-
pregnated chitosan hydrogel beads were fabricated, characterised and
employed as efficient sorbent for the Tetracycline removal from aquatic
phase. The results revealed that TC adsorption was significantly in-
creased by the addition of TCMA, and the best adsorption efficiency
was achieved in the pH range of 5–11. The pseudo-first order and Lang-
muir models were found as the best adsorption kinetic and isotherms
models, respectively, for adsorption of TC onto CS-TCMA hydrogel
beads.
