Abstract
Metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) have been attractive adsorbents for the
treatment of antibiotic wastewater. However, in their powder form, the adsorbents
suffer from the disadvantage of poor recyclability and complex operational process.
Herein, we incorporated typical MOFs (ZIF-8, ZIF-67, HKUST-1, and Fe-BTC) into
low-cost chitosan matrix for the tetracycline removal. Metal hydroxide(or metal
oxide)/chitosan composite beads from the metal salt/chitosan solution were used as
MOF precursor, which lead to the uniform and stable MOF loading into chitosan.
This composition could not only shape MOFs into beads but also improve the
adsorption capacity of the chitosan. As a result, ZIF-8-chitosan composite beads
exhibited good adsorption performance toward antibiotic tetracycline. The adsorption
processes followed pseudo-second-order model and Langmuir isotherm model. The
maximum adsorption capacity could reach 495.04 mg/g, which is higher than most of
the MOF-based or natural polymer-based tetracycline adsorbents. Moreover, the
removal efficiency of ZIF-8-chitosan composite beads toward tetracycline was still
above 90% after ten adsorption-desorption cycles. In the fixed-bed experiment, ZIF-8-
chitosan column could treat ~887 bed volumes (BV) of tetracycline solution in real
water sample. Both experimental characterizations and DFT calculations indicated
that the adsorption mechanism involves in the electrostatic interaction, π-π stacking
interaction and hydrogen-bonding interaction. This study provides a promising
strategy for uniform MOF loading into the matrix and potential adsorbents for
pharmaceutical wastewater treatment.
Keywords: Metal-organic framework; chitosan; adsorption; antibiotic; fix-bed
process

### 1. Introduction
Pharmaceutical antibiotics, as a class of emerging contaminants in the environment
matrices such as surface water and ground water, have drawn more and more attention
over the past decades [1]. They have been proven to possess various adverse effects
[2]. Owning to the excessive use, a large number of antibiotics are discharged into the
water. The antibiotics residue in environment could lead to the increase of the
resistance microbes and resistance gene, accelerating the dissemination and diffusion
of the antibiotic resistance, which potentially threatens ecosystem functions and
human health [3]. Therefore, a wide range of technologies, such as ozonation [4],
microbial degradation [5], photocatalytic degradation [6], membrane separation [7],
adsorption [8], etc., have been applied for the removal of antibiotics. Among them,
adsorption is a preferred method because of its advantages of the simplicity of design,
easy availability, and ease of operation. In addition, most of the adsorption processes
are reversible, thus the adsorbents can be easily regenerated using a suitable
desorption process for reuse. Many types of adsorbents have been utilized for the
removal of antibiotics, such as activated carbon [9], metal-based adsorbent [10],
polymeric absorbent [11] and bioadsorbent [12]. However, the developing of low-cost
and durable adsorbents with a high affinity, fast kinetics, and large capacity towards
antibiotics is still crucial.
Metal-organic frameworks (MOFs) as a novel class of emerging inorganic-organic
hybrid porous materials, which are comprised of inorganic metal ions/clusters and
coordinate organic linkers, have been investigated intensively [13]. Taking advantage
of their high surface area, exceptional porosity and easy functionalization, water-
stable MOFs have been applied as adsorbents and shown good removal abilities
toward many different pollutants from wastewater [14]. In particular, the use of MOFs

---

4
for antibiotic adsorption is also studied. Chen’s group used the zeolite imidazole
framework-8 (ZIF-8) for the removal of a mixture of two common antibiotics
(tetracycline and oxytetracycline hydrochloride) [15]. The results showed that 90.7%
of tetracycline and 82.5% of oxytetracycline hydrochloride could be removed
simultaneously. The maximum adsorption capacities for tetracycline and
oxytetracycline hydrochloride were 303.0 and 312.5 mg/g, respectively. Wang and
co-workers reported the adsorptive removal of sulfachloropyridazine by the copper-
based MOF HKUST-1 [16]. Most of the sulfachloropyridazine were removed in 15
min and the maximum adsorption capacity was 384 mg/g at 298 K. However, the
main challenge for using MOFs as adsorbents in practical applications is their powder
form. Due to the crystalline nature, they suffer from the disadvantage of weak
recyclability, complex operating process, and potential secondary pollution.
Considerable effort has been made to overcome this limitation. One solution is the
fabrication of magnetic responsive composites with the magnetic separation ability.
For example, many magnetic ZIF-8 materials have been prepared and been applied in
the environmental remediation [17-20]. The other solution is the combination of
MOFs with shapeable substrates (e.g. membranes, fibers, spheres, etc.) [21]. Synthetic
polymers are common carriers to load MOFs to prepare the shaping adsorbents [22-
24]. However, for adsorbent materials, developing low-cost substrates is still needful.
Bioadsorbents by means of natural and biodegradable polymers have become a hot
point in adsorption fields, especially from the point of view of cost, environmental
concern, and safety [25]. Cellulose and alginate have been explored as the templates
to incorporate the MOFs [26-28]. In addition to them, chitosan, a polysaccharide of
N-acetyl-d-glucosamine and d-glucosamine produced by the deacetylation of chitin, is
one of the most prominently studied natural polymers [29]. Due to the presence of

---

5
active amino and hydroxyl functional groups, chitosan has been widely studied as
potentially adsorbent for pollutants removal from water system [30]. Chitosan based
antibiotic adsorbents have also been reported in the past few years [31, 32]. However,
pure chitosan shows low adsorption capacity. The combination of MOFs with
chitosan can benefit both sides for obtaining shapeable adsorbents and improving
adsorption capacity. According to the previous reports, the MOF-polymer composites
are usually prepared from simple blending and in-situ growth [21]. The simple
blending strategy can lead to MOF particle aggregation, which reduce the adsorption
capacity. The in-situ growth strategy is a good candidate for the preparation of MOF-
chitosan composite adsorbents. However, the in-situ growth process is limited by the
substrates’ surface wettability, roughness, and charged properties. Thus, improving
the compatibility between MOFs and substrates is important. Recently, MOFs
synthesized from metal hydroxides and metal oxides have been reported [33, 34]. In
particular, chitosan/metal hydroxide(or metal oxide) composites could be obtained
from chitosan/metal salt solution [35], which can act as the precursors for uniform and
stable MOF growth onto chitosan substrates.
Taking the abovementioned into account, herein, we reported a versatile approach
to fabricate MOF-chitosan composite beads via the in-situ growth. Chitosan/metal
hydroxide(or metal oxide) beads from the acid-dissolved/alkali-solidified self-
sphering shaping method were employed as the substrates for MOFs immobilization,
in which metal hydroxide or metal oxide are the precursor templates for MOFs
growth. Metal hydroxide or metal oxide was generated from metal ions in the mixed
solution, which is uniformly in the chitosan substrate without aggregation. MOFs
were prepared from the self-conversion of corresponding metal hydroxide or metal
oxide in the organic linker solution and the obtained MOFs were immobilized in the

chitosan substrate steadily. Typical MOFs were picked to fabricate MOF-chitosan
composite beads and demonstrated the feasibility of this strategy. Moreover, the
MOF-chitosan composite beads were employed as adsorbents for the removal of
antibiotic tetracycline through batch adsorption and fixed-bed column experiments.
2. Experimental section
2.1. Materials
Chitosan (degree of deacetylation>95%, viscosity>400 mPa·s) was purchased from
Energy Chemical. Tetracycline (C22H24N2O8) was acquired from Sigma-Aldrich. 2-
Methylimidazole (C4H6N2, 98%) and trimesic acid (H3BTC, 98%) were received from
Aladdin. Other inorganic reagents were purchased from Sinopharm Chemical
Reagent Corporation. All chemicals were used as received without further purification.
2.2. Fabrication of metal hydroxide(or oxide)-chitosan composites
1.50 g of chitosan powder was dissolved in 50 mL of a 5% (w/w) aqueous solution
of acetic acid and then 10 mM metal salt (2.98 g Zn(NO3)2·6H2O, 2.42 g
Cu(NO3)2·3H2O, 4.04 g Fe(NO3)3·9H2O and 2.91 g Co(NO3)2·6H2O, respectively)
was dissolved in the chitosan solution under continuous mechanical stirring. After
they completely dissolved, the blending solution was added drop-by-drop to 300 mL
NaOH solution (1 M). After standing stirring for 5 h, the obtained beads were washed
with deionized water for several times until the elution solution approached neutral
pH. For comparison purpose, pure chitosan beads were also prepared from chitosan
solution without metal salt.
2.3. Fabrication of MOF-chitosan composites
The prepared metal hydroxide(or oxide)-chitosan composite beads were immersed
into the corresponding solutions of organic ligand under hydrothermal reaction or

solvothermal reaction. The specific experimental conditions are given in Table S1.
Then, the MOF-chitosan composite beads were obtained after washing and freeze-
drying.
2.4. Characterization
X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurements were carried out on a Rigaku SmartLab X-
ray diffractometer with Cu-Kα radiation of λ=1.5418 Å (40 kV, 30 mA). The
morphology of obtained beads was observed with field emission scanning electron
microscopy (SEM, Hitachi SU8010). Fourier-Transform infrared (FT-IR) spectra
were acquired using a Nicolet iS50 Fourier transform infrared spectrometer.
Thermogravimetric analysis (TGA) data were obtained on a Mettler toledo thermal
analyzer at a heating rate of 10 °C/min under air atmosphere. N2 adsorption-
desorption isotherms were conducted on a Quantachrome Autosorb iQ SN analyzer at
-196 °C. The point of zero charge (pHpzc) for the obtained beads was determined by
the pH-drift method. NaCl solution with the concentration of 0.1 M was used as the
electrolyte maintaining the constant ionic strength in all experiments with pH values
ranging from 1 to 12 adjusted with 0.1 M HCl or 0.1 M NaOH. 200 mg of the
obtained beads were added into 50 mL each of these solutions for 2 days. The final
pH of these solutions was measured. The pHpzc of the fiber adsorbent was accordingly
yielded based on the plot of ΔpH (pHfinal – pHinitial) against pHinitial.
2.5. Adsorption experiments
The stock solutions of tetracycline with 1000 mg/L were prepared, and then the
stock solutions were diluted to obtain the concentration of interest for adsorption
experiment. For the batch adsorption, all experiments were conducted under
mechanical stirring with a speed of 150 rpm in darkness. Tetracycline concentration
was determined by measuring the absorbances of the solutions using a UV-vis

spectroscopy (Cory 50 Conc UV0903M02) at 357 nm [36, 37]. The pH values of the
pollutant solution were adjusted by dropwise adding 0.01 mol/L NaOH or 0.01 mol/L
HCl solutions. For kinetic experiments, 50 mg adsorbent was added into 100 mL
tetracycline solution with an initial concentration of 50 mg/L, during which 3 mL
solution was collected at specific time intervals for concentration detection. The
adsorption isotherms were investigated by initial concentrations ranging from 40 to
500 mg/L with the adsorbent dosage of 0.5 g/L. For the desorption-readsorption
cycles, 10 mg adsorbent was added into 30 mL tetracycline solution (20 mg/L) for 12
h. The tetracycline-adsorbed adsorbent was regenerated by immersing into ethanol
(10 mL) for 6 h to remove adsorbed tetracycline molecules. Then, the adsorbent was
reused in adsorption experiments and the process was repeated ten times. The 10 mL
ethanol was used without the replacement during the cycles. Except for the pH effect
test, the initial pH values of tetracycline solution were adjusted as 8~9. All the

2.7. DFT calculations
To shed light on the adsorption mechanism of MOF-chitosan composite beads for
tetracycline, we evaluated the binding energies of tetracycline to chitosan and ZIF-8
using quantum chemical calculations. Here, chitosan is represented by trimer and ZIF-
8 is represented by 4-membered ring (4-MR) window in the faces and 6-membered
ring (6-MR) window in the body diagonal [38], as depicted in Scheme 1. All
calculations were carried out using the density functional theory (DFT) with the
B3LYP functional [39, 40] as implemented in the Gaussian 09 program [41]. All
geometry structures (including tetracycline, trimer, 4-MR and 6-MR) were optimized
with the 6-31G(d,p) basis set for all non-metal atoms and the LANL2DZ basis set for
Zn ion [42]. On the basis of optimized geometries, the best binding structures of
tetracycline with trimer, 4-MR and 6-MR were searched with AutoDock Vina
program [43], in which all parameters are default. Following this, all binding
structures were further optimized using B3LYP with 6-31G(d,p) basis set for non-
mental atoms and the LANL2DZ basis set for Zn ion. To obtain more accurate
energies, single-point calculations were performed based on these optimized
geometries with the 6-311G(2d,2p) basis set on all atoms except Zn, for which
LANL2DZ basis set was used. All binding energies (BE) were calculated to evaluate
the adsorption strengths of tetracycline in the different adsorption positions of MOF-
chitosan composite beads. Basis set superposition error (BSSE) was considered in the
calculations of binding energies.

3.1. Preparation and characterization of MOF-chitosan composites
The synthesis of the MOF-chitosan composites is depicted in Scheme 1. Because
of the coordination between metal ions and amino groups from chitosan, metal ions
are located into the chitosan uniformly. When the blending solution droplets come
into NaOH solution, metal hydroxide(or oxide)-chitosan composite beads are
generated due to the chitosan’s insolubility in alkali solution. Then, the metal
hydroxide(or oxide)-chitosan composite beads are transferred into the corresponding
ligand solution. Through hydrothermal or solvothermal reactions, MOFs are formed
from metal hydroxide or metal oxide in situ, which disperse into chitosan matrix
uniformly. To prove the versatility of this method, four metal salts (Zn(NO3)2·6H2O,
Cu(NO3)2·3H2O, Fe(NO3)3·9H2O and Co(NO3)2·6H2O) are selected to obtain

composite beads, Fe-based MOF-chitosan composite beads, Co-based MOF-chitosan

3.2.3. Effects of ionic strength, coexisting ions and adsorption–desorption cycles
In real wastewater, there are many salts that influence the pollutant removal. The
effect of salt ion strength was explored to show the adsorption capacity of ZIF-8-
chitosan composite beads toward tetracycline from saline wastewater. As shown in
Fig. 5d, as the concentrations of NaCl increase, the removal efficiencies of
tetracycline decrease. At high concentration of NaCl (0.5 mol/L), the adsorption
capacity decreases no more than 9%. Moreover, the effect of other coexisting ions
was also studied by adding other salts (NaNO3, Na2SO4, MgCl2, CaCl2, KCl) with the
concentration of 0.5 mol/L into the adsorption solution. Due to the competition of
coexisting ions, the adsorption capacities all show decreasing-trend (Fig. S8). SO4
2-
and Ca
2+ 
have strong impacts on tetracycline adsorption. However, the removal
efficiency is only reduced by 8.8% for SO4
2- 
and 7.7% for Ca
2+ 
even under the
concentration of 0.5 mol/L. The results suggest that ZIF-8-chitosan composite beads
have the ability to resist interference of salt ions. Additionally, reusability is also very
important in practical wastewater treatment for economic consideration. Compared
with powdery MOFs, MOF-chitosan composite beads can be recycled from the
adsorption solution with easy operational process. In this study, absolute ethanol was
used as the desorption eluent for adsorbent regeneration. The used ethanol was still
applied for the desorption in the next cycle without the replacement, which reduces
the new waste stream generation. After ten adsorption-desorption cycles, the removal
efficiency for tetracycline is still over 90% (Fig. 5e). The results show that ZIF-8-
chitosan composite beads have good reusability, which guarantees its long-term usage
in water treatment process.

To understand the adsorption mechanism, the initial solution pH value effect on the
tetracycline adsorption was conducted. The solution pH has a significant impact on
the charge of the adsorbent and tetracycline molecule. Tetracycline molecule has three
different pka at 3.3, 7.7 and 9.7, which make tetracycline include cation, zwitterion
and anion at pH ranges < 3.3, 3.3~7.6 and above 7.7, respectively (Fig. S9). The zeta
potential change of ZIF-8-chitosan composite beads was also measured at different
solution pH from 1 to 12. As shown in Fig. S10, the point of zero charge (pHzpc) is
around 9.6, which means that the surface of the composite beads is positively charged
at solution pH < pHpzc (9.6) and has a negative charge at pH > 9.6. The solution pH
effect results are shown in Fig. 6a. It can be observed that the adsorption capacity of
ZIF-8-chitosan composite beads is very low at the pH < 3.3. This is mainly due to the
strong electrostatic repulsion between ZIF-8-chitosan composite beads and
tetracycline molecules with both positively charged and the collapse of ZIF-8 [56]. At
the pH range of 3.3~7.6, tetracycline molecules become neutral, while the ZIF-8-

---

chitosan composite beads remains positive, which enhances the electrostatic attraction
between them, and thus results in the promotion of the adsorption capacity as the
increase of pH. After the solution pH increases to 7.7, the stronger electrostatic
attraction between tetracycline with negative charge and ZIF-8-chitosan composite
beads with positive charge results in that the adsorption capacity increases more
drastically. However, ZIF-8-chitosan composite beads are negatively charged at pH >
9.6. The electrostatic interactions between ZIF-8-chitosan composite beads and
tetracycline converting from strong attraction to strong repulsion with pH > 9.6 leads
to a decreasing-trend of the adsorption capacity. According to the above experimental
results, it can be inferred that the electrostatic attraction between ZIF-8-chitosan
composite beads and tetracycline should be a principal factor to drive the adsorption
of tetracycline. In addition, it can be observed that composite beads still adsorb
tetracycline when the pH is in the range (3~7.7, >9.6) which electrostatic interaction
is either weak attraction or strong repulsion. This indicates that there should be other
factors that drive the adsorption of tetracycline except for electrostatic attraction, such
as π-π stacking interactions between the aromatic rings in tetracycline and imidazole
rings in ZIF-8 [57] and hydrogen bond interactions between tetracycline and ZIF-8 or
chitosan [58]. The other factors for the tetracycline adsorption were investigated by
FT-IR analysis (Fig. 6b). The O−H or N−H (3402 cm

tetracycline such as −OH, −NHx as H-receptors and the O-containing or N-containing
groups in ZIF-8 and chitosan. The shifting of −NH2 band is attributed to the

4. Conclusion
In conclusion, we have proposed a simple strategy to incorporate MOFs into
chitosan matrix using metal hydroxide(or metal oxide)/chitosan composite beads as
MOF precursor, which show uniform MOFs distribution. Because of the composition,
ZIF-8-chitosan composite beads were shown to have excellent adsorption capacity
and good reusability toward antibiotic tetracycline. Based on the Langmuir isotherm
model, the maximum adsorption capacity could reach 495.04 mg/g, which is higher
than many of the reported adsorbents. The shape of bead could make ZIF-8-chitosan
composite be recycled easily for repeated usage. Moreover, ZIF-8-chitosan composite
beads could effectively remove tetracycline from real water sample with the fixed-bed

---

29
treatment volume of ~887 BV. The obtained results suggest that MOF-chitosan
composite can be used as a low-cost effective, easy to prepare and reusable adsorbent
for the antibiotic removal from water. In addition, this study may provide a universal
method for uniform MOF loading into different matrixes, which integrates the
properties of MOFs and matrixes for a broad range of applications, such as adsorption,
catalysts, separation, sensors, and so on.
