A B S T R A C T
A novel analytical method namely magnetic solid phase extraction in tandem with dispersive liquid-liquid
microextraction was developed and used for the extraction/preconcentration of tetracycline (TCN), oxyte-
tracycline (OTC) and doxycycline (DC) from bovine milk sample before HPLC-UV analysis. The β-cyclodextrin
functionalized silica-coated magnetic graphene oxide (Fe3O4@SiO2@GO-β-CD) was used as an adsorbent. The
adsorbent was fully characterized using FT-IR, SEM, EDX and Zeta potential techniques. The effective parameters
on the performance of the method such as extraction solvent type and volume, adsorbent amount, desorption
solvent type and volume, disperser solvent type, desorption time, ionic strength and pH value were investigated.
The limit of detection (LOD) and limit of quantification (LOQ) were obtained in the ranges of 1.8–2.9 μg L
−1 
and
6.1–9.7 μg L
−1
, respectively. The linearity was in the range of 10.0–200.0 μg L
−1 
with satisfactory determination
coefficients (R
2
) higher than 0.9929 and a good precision (RSD < 8.8%). The recovery percentages for the
analytes in real samples (bovine milk and water) were achieved in a range from 70.6 to 121.5%.
1. Introduction
Tetracyclines (TCNs), a family of broad-spectrum antibiotics, are the
second widely used in human and veterinary medicine due to their high
quality [1], low cost and side effects [2,3]. However, the incorrect
administration of TCNs results in the presence of their residues in an-
imal-based food, which poses a serious threat to consumer health (al-
lergic reactions in susceptible user, chronic toxicity, and antimicrobial
resistance) [4–6]. For protecting the human health, European Union
(EU) has set the maximum residue limit of TCs in milk as 100.0 μg L
−1
[7].
In recent decades, various analytical techniques have been estab-
lished for the determination of TCNs in milk mainly using HPLC with
various detection techniques [4,8–20], flow injection analysis, and
immunochemical techniques have been established [21,22]. However,
due to the low concentration of TCNs residues in complex matrices,
such as milk, their direct determination using chromatographic
methods is difficult [23]. Therefore, different sample preparation
techniques such as liquid-liquid extraction [11,17], hollow fiber liquid
phase microextraction [14], magnesium hydroxide co-precipitation
method [15], solid phase extraction (SPE) [9,10,24–26], molecularly
imprinted technique [27,28] and dispersive solid phase microextraction
[29] have been used to solve this problem. However, most of the tra-
ditional pretreatment methods mentioned above with more or less
limitation in practical application that was associated with labor-in-
tensive, time-consuming, operation tedious and analyte loss, usually
lead to undesirable recovery and accuracy. To overcome these draw-
backs, magnetic solid-phase extraction (MSPE) has received consider-
able attention in the recent years. In this technique, the magnetic ad-
sorbent can be easily separated from the sample solution using an
external magnetic field without the need for additional centrifugations
or filtrations steps.
Recently, graphene oxide (GO), a chemically modified graphene
sheet decorated with various oxygenated functional groups on its large
surface area [30] has attracted attentions as an MSPE sorbent. The in-
troduction of magnetic properties into GO could combine both ad-
vantages of the large surface area of GO with the convenience of the
magnetic separation; resulting in an efficient magnetic adsorbent. In
addition, in order to improve the selectivity of the extraction system
and to acquire better adsorptive properties, many studies on functio-
nalization of GO surface have been done [31]. Thus, the functionali-
zation of the adsorbent with β-CD, due to its special characteristic and

DLLME is another simple, low cost and fast microextraction tech-

2. Experimental
2.1. Reagents and materials

Stock solutions of each analyte were prepared in MeOH (1 μg mL
−1
)
and stored at 4 °C. The working mixed standard solutions of the TCNs
were prepared by diluting stock solutions with deionized water.
2.2. HPLC analysis
The HPLC analyses [33] were performed on a CXTH LC3000 chro-
matograph (Beijing, China) equipped with a UV–vis detector and a
manual injector. The chromatographic separations were carried out
using a GL Sciences Inc. C18 (250 mm × 4.6 mm, 5 μm) column at the
ambient temperature. The mobile phase consisted of acetonitrile (A)
and 25 mM KH2PO4 buffer, pH 3.0 (B). A gradient system with the
conditions of 0–10 min, 97–80% B; 10–20 min, 80–70% B; 20–55 min,
70–70% B was used. The flow rate of mobile phase was 0.7 mL min
−1
from the beginning until 10 min, then it was reduced to 0.4 mL min
−1
until the end of the run. 20.0 μL of the sample was injected into the
instrument. The detection wavelength was set at 260 nm.

2.4. Preparation of Fe3O4@SiO2@GO-β-CD
2.4.1. Synthesis of Fe3O4@SiO2-NH2 nanoparticles
Magnetic Fe3O4 (synthesized based on modified co-precipitation
method [34]) was dispersed in a water-ethanol solution (20:80, v/v).
Afterward, 15.0 mL of ammonia solution and 2.0 mL TEOS were con-
secutively added to the reaction mixture and the reaction was allowed
to stirring for 14 h at 25 °C. Then, APTES was added to the reaction
mixture, and the mixture was stirred for another 24 h at 40 °C. The si-
lica-coated magnetic nanoparticles were collected with the assistance of
an external magnet and washed several times with excess deionized
water and ethanol.
2.4.2. Synthesis of GO-β-CD
A dispersion of 0.8 g GO (synthesized based on Hummers method
[35]) in 400 mL water was ultrasonicated for 30 min in order to ex-
foliate GO. Then, 160.0 mg of EDC and 128.0 mg of NHS were added to
the exfoliated GO dispersion. Afterward, it was stirred for 30 min and
ultrasonicated for another 30 min to form a homogenous suspension.
Then 1.2 g β-CD was added to the suspension and the mixture was ul-
trasonicated for 30 min followed by stirring for 3.5 h at 60 °C. Then, the
suspension was washed with distilled water followed by centrifugation
method in order to separate the impurities and excess unreacted ma-
terials.
2.4.3. Synthesis of Fe3O4@SiO2@GO-β-CD
Finally, 0.6 g of Fe3O4@SiO2 nanoparticles was added to the GO-β-
CD suspension and ultrasonicated for 30 min. The mixture was stirred
for 1 h at 80 °C to form the final product. Fe3O4@SiO2@GO-β-CD was
collected using magnetic separation and washed with excess water for
several times, then dried in an oven (60 °C) for 30 min.
2.5. The procedure
A pasteurized low fat bovine milk from two different brands
[Choopan (M1) and Pak (M2)], a pasteurized high fat bovine milk (M3),
a sterilized low fat bovine milk (M4), and a sterilized high fat bovine
milk (M5) were purchased from a local supermarket in Tehran (Iran)
and stored refrigerated (at 4 °C) before analysis. In order to reduce the
adverse matrix effect of the milk, deproteinization is a very necessary
step before any analysis. This would prevent contamination, blockage
and irreversible adsorption of milk protein molecules onto the sta-
tionary phase of HPLC column or solid adsorbents [36,37]. Herein,
acetonitrile was used to help the removal of milk proteins in order to
determine TCNs. For every spiked milk sample, a 1:1 volume ratio of
milk (4.0 mL):acetonitrile (4.0 mL) was prepared. After manual agita-
tion for 1 min, samples were centrifuged at the speed of 3000 rpm for
10 min, then the supernatant was collected and filtered by 0.22 μm
syringes filter thereafter dilute by deionized water to 40 mL and kept
for further analysis. The prepared samples were subjected to the fol-
lowing extraction/preconcentration procedure.
At first, 10.0 mg of Fe3O4@SiO2@GO-β-CD was added to 40 mL of
the acetonitrile solution of the pretreated bovine milk spiked with the
TCNs standard solutions (50.0 and 100.0 μg L
−1
) and the mixture was
ultrasonicated for 1 min. Then, the mixture was shaken for 5 min on an
orbital shaker (300 rpm) to equilibrate the adsorption process. The
adsorbent was then separated from the aqueous phase by the assistance
of an external magnet. After the supernatant was decanted, 1 mL of
MeOH was added to the sediment and vortexed for 1.5 min in order to

3.2. The adsorption mechanism
TCNs as a guest molecule, by forming an inclusion complex can
interact with the host cavity of β-CD grafted on the surface of Fe3O4@
SiO2@GO. β-CD interacts with the aromatic ring of TCNs. The inclusion
complex formation depends mainly on the size of the guest molecule
and the binding forces, such as hydrophobic interaction, hydrogen
bonding, and Van der Waals forces, between the host cavity of β-CD and
TCNs. [39,40].
Herein, TCNs can form a strong and stable host-guest complex with
β-CD due to its chemical structure. TCNs are hydrophobic drugs con-
taining an aromatic benzene ring with the length of 5.679 Å. The cavity
depth and diameter of the β-CD are 7.9 Å and 7.8 Å, respectively. Thus,
the aromatic benzene ring of fits freely inside the β-CD cavity.
Therefore, hydrophobic-hydrophobic interactions will be the main
driving force for the formation of inclusion complex within the interior
side of the β-CD cavity as shown in Fig. 2. The presence of bulky hy-
drophilic groups such as amide and tertiary amine groups on the ali-
phatic side of TCNs do not favor the inclusion of the molecules with β-
CD cavity [31].
3.3. Optimization of magnetic SPE
3.3.1. Effect of the desorption conditions
The selection of a suitable solvent for desorbing the analytes from
the adsorbent surface is an important step in MSPE that affects the
extraction efficiency. In addition, the solvent should be compatible with
liquid chromatography and UV spectroscopy. Fe3O4@SiO2@GO-β-CD is
a hydrophilic adsorbent in nature and due to the existence of polar
groups on its structure; it can be easily dispersed in a polar solvent.
Therefore, several polar solvents including MeOH, ethanol, acetone,
and acetonitrile were tested according to the described procedure in
Section 2.5. The results in Fig. 3(a) demonstrated that the highest peak
area was obtained using MeOH. Hence, MeOH was chosen as a deso-
rption solvent for further experiments.
The effect of MeOH volume (0.8–1.8 mL) on the extraction perfor-
mance was also investigated. The results, illustrated in Fig. 3(b), show
that the maximum peak area for all TCNs was obtained using 1 mL of
MeOH. Thus, 1 mL was selected as the optimum volume of MeOH.
Moreover, desorption time as the minimum time needed for quan-
titative desorption of the analytes is another factor that affects the re-
covery of the analytes. The effect of desorption time on was studied by
changing the vortex duration from 0.25 to 3.0 min (Fig. 3(c)). As is
obvious in Fig. 3(c), the maximum efficiency was obtained at 1.5 min,
and then it remained almost constant for longer durations. Therefore, of
1.5 min was selected as desorption time in further experiments.

3.3.4. Effect of pH
The sample solution pH is a significant factor affecting the extrac-
tion performance in an MSPE process [41]. The effect of sample pH was
studied within the range of 3.0–11.0 using HCl (0.01 M) or NaOH
(0.01 M) solutions. As it is known, the TCNs are amphoteric molecules,
where ionization of exciting forms of TCNs is affected by pH of the
sample solution. Thus, TCNs could be positively charged at pH < 3.3,
zwitterion (neutral) at 3.3 < pH < 9.0, and negatively charged at
pH > 9.0 [42].
As is illustrated in Fig. 1D, the adsorbent's isoelectric point is be-
tween pH 3.0 and 4.0, at which the zeta potential shifts from positive to
negative, indicating that the surface of Fe3O4@SiO2@GO-β-CD is ne-
gatively charged for pHs > pHZPC. The results in Fig. 3(f) indicate that
the peak areas increase with the pH increasing from 3.0 to 7.0. After
that, at the pH values > 7.0 the peak areas slightly decreases. This
could be interpreted as follows: As at pH < 3.3, both TCNs species and
the adsorbent surface are positively charged, the electrostatic repulsion
between two positive charges is the robust force. However, there is still
a considerable amount of TCNs adsorbed on the Fe3O4@SiO2@GO-β-
CD surface, indicating that besides the electrostatic interactions, a hy-
drophobic interaction (host-guest) between the cavity of β-CD and the
benzene rings in TCNs molecules is occurring. When the pH of the so-
lution increases above pH 3.3, the extraction efficiency increases to
reach its highest value at pH 7.0. Since at pH 6.0–7.0, the TCNs are in
their neutral form (no charge), and the adsorbent surface is negatively
charged, the lowest electrostatic repulsion is occurring at pH 6.0–7.0,
leading to the maximum extraction efficiency. As the pH increases
further than 7.7 the dominant species of TCNs become the two nega-
tively charged ones (TCNs
− 
and TCNs
2−
) [43] which form a strong
electrostatic repulsion with the negatively charged surface of the ad-
sorbent. Then, the extraction efficiency will decrease as shown in
Fig. 3(f). Thus, pH 7.0 was selected for the subsequent experiments.
3.3.5. Effect of ionic strength
Salt addition to the sample solution can increase or decrease the
solubility of the analytes through salting-in and salting-out, respec-
tively. Where in some cases it has no effect on the extraction recovery.
In this study, the effect of salt concentration was tested by the addition
of different amounts of NaCl (in the range of 0.0–5.0% w/v) into the
sample solution. As shown in Fig. 3(g), the peak areas for the three
TCNs sharply decreased as the amount of NaCl increased. This can be
explained by the fact that the sodium ions can form an inclusion
complex with the β-CD cavity, thus, at high salt contents, the bonded
sodium ions reduce the interaction sites on the surface of Fe3O4@
SiO2@GO-β-CD resulting in lower extraction recoveries. Hence, further
experiments were carried out without any salt added.
3.3.6. Effect of sample volume
The effect of sample volume was also investigated to achieve reli-
able analytical results and a high preconcentration factor. In this study,
the sample volume was optimized by studying its effect in the range of
10–100 mL. The results depicted in Fig. 3(h) shows that the efficiency
enhanced with increasing the sample solution volume from 10.0 to
40.0 mL and after that decreased. The reduction efficiency at the vo-
lumes higher than 40.0 mL can be due to the dilution effect. Therefore,
40 mL was selected for the subsequent experiments.

3.4. Optimization of DLLME
3.4.1. Effect of type and volume of extraction solvent
The selection of an appropriate extraction solvent in DLLME method
is of critical importance. The extraction solvent should have the fol-
lowing merits: a density lower than water (as a good way to avoid the
usage of chlorinated solvents), low water solubility, a high extraction
efficiency, no interference with the analyte, being cheap and of the
highest purity. Based on these facts, toluene, octanol, n-hexane, and
cyclohexane were examined for this study. As can be seen in Fig. 4(a),
among these solvents, n-hexane showed the highest extraction effi-
ciency for the target analytes. Hence, it was selected as the extraction
solvent for subsequent experiments.
To investigate the effect of extraction solvent volume, 1 mL of
MeOH and different volumes of n-hexane (30.0, 40.0, 50.0, and
60.0 μL) were added to 10 mL of deionized water. Fig. 4(b) shows that
the peak areas increased with the increase of n-hexane to reach the
maximum at 40.0 μL and after that, it remained approximately con-
stant. Thereby, 40.0 μL n-hexane was selected as the optimum volume
of extraction solvent for further experiments.
3.4.2. The effect of aqueous phase volume
In the DLLME process, the volume ratio of the extraction and dis-
perser solvents and the aqueous phase influence the extraction effi-
ciency. The effect of the volume of deionized water on the extraction
efficiency was studied in the range of 5.0–20.0 mL with 1.0 mL MeOH
as the disperser solvent and 40.0 μL of n-hexane as the extraction sol-
vent. The results showed that the extraction efficiency increased as the
volume of deionized water increased up to 10.0 mL, and then slightly
decreased (Fig. 4(c)). Therefore, 10.0 mL of deionized water was chosen
for the next experiments.
3.4.3. Effect of extraction time
The influence of extraction time was studied over the range of
0–4 min. The results in Fig. 4(d) indicates that the extraction efficiency
increased by increasing the vortex time to 1 min, and more prolonged
vortex time did not provide any increase in the extraction efficiency.
Hence, 1 min was selected as the extraction time in the subsequent
experiments.
3.5. Reusability
Reusability of the adsorbent is a very critical aspect of extraction
procedure from the economic and environmental points of view. In
order to examine the reusability of Fe3O4@SiO2@GO-β-CD nano-ad-
sorbent, the used adsorbent was washed with sufficient amounts of
acetone and deionized water (2 times). Then, it was reused for the next
MSPE-DLLME procedure according to Section 2.5. Based on the ob-
tained results, the adsorbent can be reused at least 15 times by < 5%
loss in the extraction recoveries. The results indicate that Fe3O4@
SiO2@GO-β-CD has a great potential for being repeatedly used in
sample preparation procedures.

3.8. Comparison study
The performance of the developed MSPE-DLLME/HPLC-UV
method based on Fe3O4@SiO2@GO-β-CD as adsorbent was compared
with other reported methods [14,19,20,22,25–27,29]. As can be seen
in Table 3, the presented work showed a shorter extraction time
(5 min) than the previous works, except in [29], the extraction time
was 30 s. However, they used 30 mg of adsorbent, while in this work
only 10.0 mg was used. This could be related to the effective inter-
actions between the analytes and the adsorbent. The method showed
an acceptable linearity, a higher EF, a low RSD% and detection limits,
and good recovery values compared with previously reported
methods. The reported detection limit in [20] was lower than this
work, nevertheless, it had been applied for extraction of TCNs from
water samples and not from bovine milk. Additionally, this method
has a higher EF (667) when compared with other published methods.
Such results indicate that the procedure is fast, simple and sensitive,
suggesting that Fe3O4@SiO2@GO-β-CD is a favorable adsorbent for
MSPE-DLLME/HPLC-UV, having a great ability toward the extraction
of TCNs in milk samples.
4. Conclusions
The MSPE method combined with DLLME was developed as a new
approach for extraction of TCNs (OTC, TCN, and DC) in bovine milk
sample using Fe3O4@SiO2@GO-β-CD nanocomposite prior to HPLC-
UV determination. This provided selective and sensitive determina-
tion of the analytes in complex matrices. Compared with other
conventional sample preparation methods, the proposed technique
offers several advantages such as high EF, relatively short analysis
time (5 min), low LOD (1.8–2.9 μg L
−1
), favorable reproducibility,
low consumption of organic solvents and satisfactory relative re-
coveries.
