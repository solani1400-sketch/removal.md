a b s t r a c t
The dairy industry disposes of a large volume of waste milk with antibiotic residues, which is a great
cause of much concern in soil and water environments. In this study, the electrochemical oxidation
of tetracycline antibiotics (TCs) in cow’s milk was investigated. Milk contains a high concentration of
organic matter, and the concentrations of TCs residues are extremely low. The effects of anode materials
and electrolytes on the degradation of oxytetracycline (OTC) were investigated. A higher degradation
rate for the OTC was attained using the inactive anode or a NaCl electrolyte. It was found that a physically
adsorbed oxidant on the surface of the anode and indirect oxidation using electrogenerated hypochlorite
could enhance the degradation of OTC in raw milk. The organic components in milk samples affected the
removal rate of the OTC. The removal rate constants for the OTC in raw milk were 2.8–7.7 times higher
than the chemical oxygen demand values. It was found that electrochemical oxidation could decompose
low concentrations of TCs in high concentrations of organic matter solutions selectively. The results
indicate that electrochemical oxidation is an effective method for the treatment of TCs in waste milk.

1. Introduction
Large amounts of various antibiotics are used routinely in veteri-
nary therapy for the prevention and control of disease. More than
2200 tons of antibiotics per year are used in Japan, with approx-
imately 60% of these used in the livestock industry. Mastitis is
one of the representative diseases in dairy cows and remains the
most costly disease. It reduces milk yield and affects the qual-
ity of the milk in all dairy-producing countries in the world [1].
It is estimated that between 2 and 55% of all lactating cows will
include a mastitis infection [2]. The incidence rate of mastitis
is frequently increased to more than 10%, and production losses
caused by subclinical mastitis cost the dairy industry $1 billion
annually [3]. Clinical mastitis in dairy cows is commonly treated
with antibiotics directly via intramammary infusions and injec-
tions, which produces waste milk with antibiotic residues. It was
reported that cows infused with 200 mg of antibiotic produced
milk containing the antibiotic at 44.2 g/ml [4]. During the with-
drawal period, the milk from cows that have been treated with
an antibiotic must be withheld from sale. This waste milk rep-
resents an economic loss and causes disposal problems [5]. The
feeding of milk with antibiotic residues to calves is prohibited

because it has been reported that antibiotic-containing milk has
poor palatability and is often rejected by calves, slows the growth
rates of the calves, their antibiotics may select for resistance in the
calves’ enteric flora, and increases the risk of disease [2,6]. 1342
million liters of waste milk containing antibiotics are disposed of
annually in the UK [6]. This leads to problems with the spread
of antibiotic-resistant microorganisms to environmental soil and
water. Therefore, it is necessary to develop appropriate technology
for the degradation of the antibiotics in waste milk. Aerobic and
anaerobic treatments are ineffective in the treatment of antibiotic
residues in milk because of bacterial inhibition. Electrochemical
oxidation is a physico-chemical method for the degradation of
biorefractory substances. Electrochemical processes are being used
for the treatment of wastewater and the decomposition of some
compounds [7,8]. Miyata et al. [9] successfully applied electro-
chemical oxidation to the degradation of tetracycline antibiotics
(TCs) in aqueous solution.
In this study, the electrochemical oxidation of tetracycline
antibiotics in milk was investigated using both active and inactive
anodes with different electrolytes. Milk contains a variety of con-
stituents, including lactose, casein, and milk fat, with high chemical
oxygen demand (COD) values of more than 100,000 mg/L. Electro-
chemical treatment has the problem of requiring large amounts
of energy. Selective oxidation is effective at reducing the energy
consumption. The objective of this work was to develop an electro-
chemical oxidation method for the selective degradation of TCs in
milk.

2. Materials and methods
2.1. Chemicals
Fig. 1 shows the chemical structure and molecular weights of
the tetracycline antibiotics. Oxyteracycline hydrochloride and ace-
tonitrile (LC–MS grade) were obtained from Sigma (St. Louis, MO,
USA). Methanol (HPLC grade) and other reagents were purchased
from Wako (Osaka, Japan).
2.2. Preparation of solutions
For the electrochemical experiments, raw cow’s milk, whole
milk, and fat-free milk were prepared. The whole milk was obtained
at a grocery store in Japan. The raw milk was obtained from a dairy
farm located in Kansai, Japan. The fat-free milk was prepared by
centrifuging raw milk at 6000 rpm for 30 min (15 
◦
C) to attain a
milk fat ratio of less than 0.5%. Thirty mg of each antibiotic was
added to 300 ml of milk to prepare sample solutions. In order to
study the effect of the initial concentration, the experiments were
conducted using 10, 20, and 100 mg/L of oxytetracycline (OTC) in
raw milk.
2.3. Electrochemical experiment
The electrochemical oxidation experiment was conducted in a
glass beaker, equipped with a 150 mm × 50 mm mesh anode and
a plate cathode. The anode material was a Ti/IrO2 (iridium oxide
doped titanium) or Ti/PbO2 (lead oxide doped titanium) electrode.
A titanium plate (150 mm × 50 mm) was used as the cathode. The
anode and cathode were placed vertically and parallel to each other
with an electrode gap of 5 mm in a glass beaker. One thousand mg
of Na2SO4 or NaCl was added as the electrolyte. The electrochemi-
cal oxidation was carried out at a constant current of 1.5 A using a
DC power supply. The sample solution was agitated by a magnetic
stirrer.

2.4.2. COD measurement
The chemical oxygen demand (COD) was determined using a
dichromate method. A UV/Vis spectrophotometer (DR4000, HACH,
USA) was used to measure the COD value.
3. Results and discussion
3.1. Electrochemical oxidation of tetracycline antibiotics in raw
milk
The electrochemical oxidation of the TCs, tetracycline (TC),
chlortetracycline (CTC), and OTC was investigated in raw milk using
a Ti/IrO2 anode with Na2SO4 electrolyte (Fig. 2). The results show
that the concentrations of these TCs in raw milk were reduced.
The concentrations of the TCs were reduced from 100 mg/L to 5.2,
4.7, and 4.2 mg/L for TC, CTC, and OTC, respectively, during the 6-h
electrochemical treatment. The three TCs showed similar degrada-
tion properties. The results showed that electrochemical oxidation
using a Ti/IrO2 anode is an efficient method for decomposing TCs
in raw milk.

3.2. Effect of anode materials on degradation of OTC in raw milk
The effect of the anode material on the electrochemical oxida-
tion for the degradation of OTC was tested in raw milk. The initial
OTC concentration was 100 mg/L. Fig. 3 shows the results for the
OTC concentration using a Ti/IrO2 anode compared with a Ti/PbO2
anode during the electrochemical oxidation with the Na2SO4 elec-
trolyte. When using a Ti/IrO2 anode, 83% of the OTC was removed
during the 6-h electrochemical treatment. With a Ti/PbO2 anode,
93% of the OTC was removed during the 6-h treatment. A higher
OTC degradation rate was obtained with Ti/PbO2. The difference
between Ti/IrO2 and Ti/PbO2 could be explained by the different
two states for “active oxygen” at the anode surface with direct
anodic oxidation [12]. The physically adsorbed “active oxygen”
(
•
OH) can oxidize organic compounds at the surface of an inactive
electrode such as Ti/SnO2 or Ti/PbO2 [13]. In contrast, the chemi-
cally adsorbed “active oxygen” tends to be oxidized into O2 with an
active electrode such as Ti/IrO2. Thus, it can favor the partial oxi-
dation of organic compounds with an active electrode. The results
indicated that the anode material affects the degradability of OTC
in raw milk, and the physically adsorbed “active oxygen” at the
surface of a Ti/PbO2 anode accelerated the degradation of OTC.
3.3. Effect of electrolytes on degradation of OTC in raw milk
The effect of the electrolyte on the degradation of OTC in raw
milk was investigated using Na2SO4 or NaCl as an electrolyte
with a Ti/IrO2 anode (Fig. 4). The initial concentration of OTC was
100 mg/L. With the Na2SO4 electrolyte, 83% of the OTC was removed
during the 6-h treatment. With the NaCl electrolyte, more than
99.2% of the OTC was removed during the 5-h electrochemical
treatment. After 6-h, the concentration was less than the detection
limit.
The electrochemical oxidation is explained by a direct anodic
oxidation and/or an indirect anodic oxidation [14]. In direct anodic
oxidation, the organic matter is destroyed on the oxide anode by
electrochemical conversion or combustion [12]. In indirect oxi-
dation, the electrogenerated oxidants such as hypochlorite [15]
destroy the organic matter in the bulk solution among the elec-
trodes. With the Na2SO4 electrolyte, the direct oxidation mainly
occurs at the anode surface. With the NaCl electrolyte, the direct
oxidation occurs at the anode surface and indirect oxidation also

3.4. Comparison of removal rates between OTC and organic

Electric power consumption is a major cost factor for the elec-
trochemical process.
It is preferred that the electrochemical treatment has the ability
to selectively oxidize OTC in aquatic solutions with organic mat-
ter in order to reduce the energy consumption of the treatment.
Milk is an aquatic solution with a high concentration of organic
matter. The concentration of COD was determined as an index of
the concentration of organic matter. Fig. 5 shows the concentration
changes for COD and OTC during the electrochemical oxidation in
raw milk. The COD values were reduced by 19.8–62.3% during the
6-h treatment. In addition to the OTC, the components of the raw
milk sample were also considered to be degraded.

3.7. Effect of initial concentration of OTC in raw milk
The effect of the initial OTC concentration (10–100 mg/L) on the
electrochemical oxidation process was investigated using a Ti/IrO2
anode with the Na2SO4 electrolyte in raw milk (Fig. 8). OTC removal
efficiencies of 70, 81, and 83% were achieved at the initial concen-
trations of 10, 20, and 100 mg/L, respectively. The OTC rate constant
showed no significant differences between these initial concentra-
tions. The initial concentration of OTC slightly affected the removal
rate and rate constant of the OTC. At a low initial concentration, the
OTC degradation rate became smaller, and more oxidants acted to
degrade the components of the raw milk.
4. Conclusions
The electrochemical oxidation of TCs in raw milk was investi-
gated for waste milk treatment. A high OTC degradation rate was
achieved using a Ti/PbO2 anode or NaCl electrolyte. The results indi-
cated that oxygen physically adsorbed at the surface of an inactive
Ti/PbO2 electrode and indirect anodic oxidation using NaCl accel-
erated the degradation of OTC. Electrochemical oxidation could be
adapted to degrade a small amount of TCs in high concentrations
of organic matter solutions. The rate constants showed that OTC
was degraded selectively in raw milk. Although the electrochemi-
cal oxidation has so far only been demonstrated in the laboratory,
selective oxidation could play an important role in turning electro-
chemical oxidation into a useful treatment method. The selective
degradation of antibiotics in raw milk could be an advantage in
the treatment method. The results suggest that electrochemical
oxidation is an effective method for the treatment of tetracycline
antibiotic residues in raw milk to avoid environmental contamina-
tion.
